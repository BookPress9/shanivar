# Technique 04: The Fading Tone

## Primary Source
- **Verse:** Vijñāna-bhairava-tantra, verse 41.
- **Translation cited:** Christopher Wallis ("Hareesh"), *A Translation of the Vijñāna-bhairava-tantra* (2017, rev. 2018, hareesh.org): the verse describes one "whose heart-mind is completely focused on the prolonged sounds of a musical instrument such as a *tantrī* [a stringed instrument like a vīṇā], through the duration of the phases [of their resonance], at the limit [of the perceptible sound]."
- **Cross-checked against:** An independently-worded rendering found via secondary sourcing describes the same verse as instructing sustained attention to "the sound of a stringed instrument... through the duration of the phases of their resonance," tracking the tone as it moves from something like a bell's ring, through subtler stages, down to the faintest audible trace — different phrasing from Wallis's, consistent content, confirming both the verse's subject matter and its number.
- **What the verse says:** Fix full attention on a single sustained musical tone (the example given is a plucked string) and follow it continuously as it decays — not just at the loud onset, but all the way down through its fading resonance to the edge of audibility.

## Essence
Most listening is aimed at the loudest, most obvious part of a sound. This technique redirects listening toward the opposite end: the long, quiet decay most people stop attending to as soon as a sound stops being "interesting." Following a tone all the way to silence asks for a kind of sustained, effortless attention that doesn't need a strong stimulus to stay engaged.

## Guided Session Script
*(Adaptation — modern secular framing. Approximately 5 minutes. Requires one resonant sound source: a singing bowl, a struck glass, a tuning fork, a guitar or piano string, or a recording of one.)*

Sit where you can hear clearly, ideally somewhere quiet. Have your sound source ready — strike it, pluck it, or play the recording once you're settled.

Close your eyes. Produce the tone (or start the recording), and let your attention land fully on the sound the moment it begins.

Follow the tone continuously as it changes — don't just note "there's a sound" and drift off. Track how it shifts: from full and bright, to softer, to thinner, all the way down to the quietest edge where you're not sure anymore whether you're still hearing it or just remembering it.

When the tone fades below hearing, stay with that edge for a moment — the point where sound and silence are hard to tell apart.

Produce the tone again (or replay it), and repeat the same close tracking, start to finish.

Each time, try to catch the tone a little earlier in its decay and follow it a little further than before.

After the tone has fully faded for the last time, stay seated in the resulting quiet before opening your eyes — see if the surrounding silence sounds any different than it did before you started.

## Modern Adaptation Notes
- Dropped: the verse's specific endpoint — that the practitioner's "form becomes that of the formless supreme Void" — replaced with a plain, checkable instruction (follow the tone to inaudibility, then rest in the quiet) with no metaphysical claim about identity or dissolution.
- Kept: the instrument-agnostic core of the practice. The verse names a stringed instrument as an example; we generalized to "any resonant, decaying tone" (bowl, bell, tuning fork) since the mechanism — sustained tracking of a fading sound — doesn't depend on the specific instrument, and a stringed instrument isn't always available to a modern user.
- Added (adaptation only): the closing instruction to sit in silence after the last tone and notice the surrounding quiet — a natural extension of the verse's logic, not present in the verse itself, included to give the practice a clear, gentle landing point.

4–6 minutes
