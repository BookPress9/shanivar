# Technique 02: Resting in the Pause

## Primary Source
- **Verse:** Vijñāna-bhairava-tantra, verse 27.
- **Translation cited:** Christopher Wallis ("Hareesh"), *A Translation of the Vijñāna-bhairava-tantra* (2017, rev. 2018, hareesh.org).
- **What the verse says:** Where verse 24 describes the *momentary* pause at each end of the breath, verse 27 turns to the stillness after the exhale or after the inhale and calls it "quiescent" or "tranquil" (*śānta*).

## Essence
This technique takes the same pause explored in Technique 01 and asks the practitioner to stay in it slightly longer — not by force, but by choice. Where Technique 01 is about *noticing* an already-brief gap, this one is about *staying with* it just enough to feel its texture.

## Guided Session Script
*(Adaptation — modern secular framing. Approximately 5 minutes.)*

Sit comfortably, spine tall but not rigid. Rest your hands in your lap. Close your eyes if that's comfortable.

Breathe normally, without counting or changing the breath. Each time the breath reaches one of its natural pauses — after the in-breath or after the out-breath — let your attention stay in that stillness a little longer, without holding the breath to make the pause last. Let each pause be a place to rest, not a place to push through.

After a while, notice if anything in your body has started to settle — the shoulders, the jaw, the space between your eyebrows. You're not trying to make this happen; you're only noticing if it does.

Toward the end, breathe however feels natural, and see if the same settled quality you found in the pauses is still available, even without deliberately creating it.

Open your eyes when you're ready.

## Modern Adaptation Notes
- Adapted as attention only: the breath stays ordinary. The practitioner does not hold, lengthen, count or pace the breath, and only rests attention in the pauses that occur on their own.
- Dropped: any reading of the verse as a practice of controlling the breath, and any suggestion that a special breath experience is something to seek or expect.
- Dropped: identification of the result with "the Tranquil One [Śiva]" manifesting. The script only invites the practitioner to notice whether anything has settled, without promising an outcome.

## Suggested Duration
4–6 minutes
