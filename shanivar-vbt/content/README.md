# Shanivar VBT — Content Index

12 Level-1 techniques sourced from the *Vijñāna-bhairava-tantra* (Kashmir Śaiva text, c. 850 CE per Christopher Wallis's dating; other scholars place it as early as the 5th–9th century), selected for being breath-based, sensory, or attention-based enough to render in a guided modern format without requiring unsafe postures, obscure ritual apparatus, or heavy doctrinal explanation.

**Primary translation used throughout:** Christopher Wallis ("Hareesh"), *A Translation of the Vijñāna-bhairava-tantra* (complete but lacking commentary), self-published 2017, revised 2018, distributed via hareesh.org. Wallis is a Sanskrit scholar (D.Phil., Oxford) and author of *Tantra Illuminated* and *The Recognition Sūtras*; this is a direct scholarly translation from the Sanskrit. Every technique below was cross-checked, where possible, against at least one independently-worded second source (most often consistent with Jaideva Singh's *Vijñānabhairava or Divine Consciousness: A Treasury of 112 Types of Yoga*, Motilal Banarsidass, 1979, or Osho's *The Book of Secrets* commentary) — see each technique file's "Primary Source" section for the specific cross-check used and any numbering caveats.

**A note on verse numbering:** The Sanskrit text is ~162 verses total, of which verses 24–139 present the ~112 dhāraṇās (techniques). Different translators/commentators occasionally split or merge adjacent half-verses differently, which can shift a given technique's cited number by one or a few across editions.

**Canonical numbering policy (set after QA review):** Displayed verse numbers always mean the Sanskrit *sloka* number per Wallis/Singh — never an informal "Technique # of 112" ordinal count used by some non-scholarly VBT sites. QA traced the Technique 09/10 numbering flag to exactly this units mismatch (sloka number vs. ordinal position), not a real scholarly edition dispute — see `shanivar-vbt/qa/citation-verification-report.md`. Any future contributor cross-referencing a different VBT website against a number here should check this policy before re-raising it as a discrepancy.

**Category tags** use the same 7-code vocabulary as the Personalization/Assessment Agent's routing table (`shanivar-vbt/assessment/routing-table.md`): `BR` breath-based, `SO` sound-based, `VI` visual-gazing, `BS` body-sensation, `GT` gap-between-thoughts, `SA` spatial-awareness, `EM` emotion-based.

## Summary for QA / Staff Manager

- **12 of 12 techniques sourced**, each with a named verse number and a named translation edition (Christopher Wallis's 2017/2018 scholarly translation, cited throughout), per the hard citation requirement.
- **12 of 12 fully verified** (updated by QA/Scriptural Integrity Agent, 2026-09-08) with a strong primary-source quote plus at least one independently-worded second source (in every case, Jaideva Singh's *Vijñānabhairava or Divine Consciousness*, Motilal Banarsidass, 1979, full text located and checked directly via archive.org) confirming the same verse content and number.
- **The 3 previously-flagged items were resolved by QA**, not merely re-confirmed:
  - **Technique 07** (The Gentle Swing, verse 83): Jaideva Singh's translation, checked directly, numbers this practice verse 83 too — exact match to Wallis on both number and content ("owing to the swinging of the body... his mental state becomes calmed... he attains *divyaugha*"). Flag cleared.
  - **Technique 09** (The Gap Between Two, verses 61–62): Jaideva Singh's translation matches Wallis exactly at verses 61 and 62, both number and content. Flag cleared. The earlier "numbering variance" traced to secondary/popular sources conflating an ordinal technique-count (1st–112th) with the Sanskrit sloka number — a units mismatch, not a real scholarly-edition disagreement. Standardize on verses 61–62.
  - **Technique 10** (Threshold of Sleep, verse 75): Jaideva Singh's translation matches Wallis exactly at verse 75, both number and content. The "alt. cited elsewhere as 62" is the same units-mismatch phenomenon as Technique 09 — at least one non-scholarly compilation site was found explicitly labeling this content "Technique 62" while itself stating "this is verse 75 in the Sanskrit text." No scholarly translation consulted places this content at sloka 62 (that sloka belongs to Technique 09's "gap between two thoughts" continuation instead). Flag cleared. Standardize on verse 75; the "alt. 62" note has been removed from this technique's title/index entry as unfounded.
- See `shanivar-vbt/qa/citation-verification-report.md` for the full verification report, spot-check notes on the other 9 techniques, the overclaim-language scan, and the release-gate decision.
- **No techniques were dropped mid-process** from this final list of 12, but a number of well-known VBT techniques were considered and deliberately excluded during sourcing for reasons that map directly to the task's exclusion criteria:
  - Explicit sexual/tantric-union practices (verses ~65–70) — excluded as requiring heavy doctrinal framing and being unsuitable for a general secular product.
  - Named hand *mudrās* (e.g., *ṣaṇmukhī*, *khecarī*, *karankinī*) and multi-chakra visualization sequences (verses ~28–37) — excluded as requiring specialist embodied instruction to perform correctly and a subtle-body cosmology to make sense of, which risks losing essence rather than preserving it in a text-only guided format.
  - Whirling until falling to the ground (verse 111) and piercing the body with a needle (verse 93) — excluded on physical safety grounds.
  - Skull/cranium-focused contemplation (verse 34) and cremation-ground/body-burning visualizations (verses 52–53) — excluded as carrying memento-mori/funerary associations likely to read as morbid or alarming without extensive framing, inconsistent with the product's non-preachy, non-frightening tone constraint.
- All 12 guided scripts are original adaptations written for this product, clearly separated in each file from the cited primary-source verse content, per the task's requirement not to present paraphrase as primary text.
