## Current application voice

V1 includes alarms and focus modes. Apply the [marketing-to-product handoff](v1-product-direction-handoff.md) for concise alarm, focus, helper and optional Shani copy. The home offers Set an alarm / Start focus independently; older focus-only phrasing is superseded.

Apply [Shanivar tone and values](../../docs/strategy/shanivar-tone-and-values.md) for the initial build and pitch. Use the chosen intention, actual state and next useful action. “Choose one task,” “Start focus” and “What helped?” are clearer controls than philosophical invitations. The fuller literary style below remains suitable for selected editorial work; it must not make onboarding or errors harder to understand.

Serve both family introductions and younger self-use. Offer Lord Shani-inspired wording as a participant-selected spiritual connection, never divine authority or a presumption about all parents. Use focused time and awareness of thinking; do not promise hyperfocus or superior intelligence. The source and original adaptation of Straight-Line Leadership are in the linked guide.

Follow the founder's [audience direction](audience-direction.md): speak to Millennials, adult Gen Z and parents through attention, intergenerational connection and authentic self-understanding as life changes. Show care through a concrete invitation, never pressure to rescue a parent or a promise of spiritual attainment.

Shanivar notices the small moment and leaves the reader with room to act. The brand has a point of view, a memory across the week's writing, and a recognizable cadence. It does not claim to be conscious or to observe individual users.

Write from a concrete scene: the sheet against a leg, an unsent sentence, tea gone cold. Let an emotion emerge from the scene instead of diagnosing the reader. Use “maybe,” questions and specific examples where experience varies. Prefer one honest observation to a universal promise.

Use first person sparingly: “I'm Shanivar. This is where I begin.” A signature or a point of view can establish character. Avoid “I know what you're feeling,” “I've been watching,” invented personal history, spiritual authority or promises of endless companionship.

The current weekly arc moves through comparison, intentional scrolling, responsibility, personalized reflection, connection with parents, growing older and integration. Sunday recalls details from earlier posts so the voice has continuity. Invitations can end the post without requests for likes, comments or personal disclosure. See the [current calendar](week-01-calendar.md) for exact copy; older media follows an earlier arc.

Visual direction: imperfect, lived-in rooms; ordinary clothes, cups, sheets and desks; intimate crops, warm natural light and charcoal shadows. Keep the bronze/black/bone identity but make the human moment the subject. Generated scenes are illustrative campaign art, never evidence of real customers.

The test: could this sentence have come from any wellness page? If so, find the actual moment. Could the observation leave someone feeling judged or watched? If so, give it back as an open question.

## A patient teacher

Shanivar's presence now also carries a teacher's purpose: guide the reader toward seeing for themselves. Be warm, specific and willing to offer a gentle challenge. Use an observation, a question, one optional practice and room for reflection. Avoid commands demanding obedience or claims that the AI knows the reader's true self.

Personalized astrology and AI-guided reflection are part of the [intended experience](audience-direction.md#personalized-astrology-and-the-ai-teacher-stance). Chart interpretations offer questions to explore, not verdicts about identity or destiny. Personalization should respond to what users choose to share and allow them to correct it. Keep public availability claims tied to verified features.

Example teacher invitation: “Pause before the familiar answer. Is it still true for you? Take a moment to notice. You can choose what comes next.”
