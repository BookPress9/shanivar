# Technique 02: Tranquil Retention

## Primary Source
- **Verse:** Vijñāna-bhairava-tantra, verse 27.
- **Translation cited:** Christopher Wallis ("Hareesh"), *A Translation of the Vijñāna-bhairava-tantra* (2017, rev. 2018, hareesh.org). Also cross-checked against Wallis's separate 2022 blog post "Vijñāna-bhairava-tantra verses 22–27: the foundational practice" (hareesh.org), which independently renders the same verse: the breath-power, "called 'quiescent' [śānta]," settles briefly after an exhale or an inhale, and in that stillness "the Tranquil One [Śiva] manifests."
- **What the verse says:** Where verse 24 describes the *momentary* pause at each end of the breath, verse 27 describes deliberately extending that pause — a gentle retention (Sanskrit *kumbhita*) after the exhale or after the inhale — until the breath's motion settles into a distinctly still, "tranquil" quality. Wallis's commentary notes this can be practiced with a simple even-count ratio (e.g., a 1:1:1:1 breath: inhale, hold, exhale, hold, each for an equal count).

## Essence
This technique takes the same pause explored in Technique 01 and asks the practitioner to stay in it slightly longer — not by force, but by choice, using a simple counted ratio as a scaffold. Where Technique 01 is about *noticing* an already-brief gap, this one is about *extending* it just enough to feel its texture: the sense of the nervous system settling when the breath itself is momentarily suspended.

## Guided Session Script
*(Adaptation — modern secular framing. Approximately 5 minutes.)*

Sit comfortably, spine tall but not rigid. Rest your hands in your lap. Close your eyes if that's comfortable.

We're going to breathe in a simple, even pattern: in for a count of four, hold gently for four, out for four, hold gently for four. If four feels like a strain, use three instead — the count matters less than the evenness.

Breathe in... two, three, four.
Hold... two, three, four. No straining — just a soft, comfortable pause.
Breathe out... two, three, four.
Hold... two, three, four.

Continue that cycle at your own pace for the next several rounds. Let each hold be a place to rest, not a place to push through.

Around the third or fourth round, notice if anything in your body has started to settle — the shoulders, the jaw, the space between your eyebrows. You're not trying to make this happen; you're only noticing if it does.

If at any point the count feels forced or you feel short of breath, drop the holds entirely and just breathe normally for a few cycles before returning to the pattern, or stop the counted pattern altogether and simply breathe.

For the last minute, let go of the counting. Breathe however feels natural, and see if the same settled quality you found during the holds is still available, even without deliberately creating it.

Open your eyes when you're ready.

## Modern Adaptation Notes
- Dropped: the verse's ambiguity between *voluntary* breath retention and *spontaneous* kumbhaka that Wallis flags as arising involuntarily in advanced meditators with "awakened kuṇḍalinī." We adapted only the voluntary, self-paced version — a mild counted retention — and dropped any implication that a spontaneous breath-stoppage experience is something to seek or expect. This is also a safety consideration: we do not want users attempting extended or forceful breath retention, especially first-time practitioners.
- Added (adaptation, not in source): an explicit 4-4-4-4 count as a concrete on-ramp, since the original verse does not specify a ratio — Wallis suggests this as one plausible practical interpretation, not as part of the verse text itself. This is clearly a bridge we built, not something Bhairava said.
- Added (safety note, adaptation only): an explicit instruction to drop the holds if breathless or straining — not present in the source text, included because breath-retention practices carry real risk of dizziness or hyperventilation-adjacent discomfort if pushed, and the source verse gives no safety caveat since it assumes an experienced practitioner under a teacher's guidance.
- Dropped: identification of the result with "the Tranquil One [Śiva]" manifesting — replaced with plain physiological language ("settle").
