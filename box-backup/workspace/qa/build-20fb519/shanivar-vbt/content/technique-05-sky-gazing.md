# Technique 05: Sky Gazing

## Primary Source
- **Verse:** Vijñāna-bhairava-tantra, verse 84.
- **Translation cited:** Christopher Wallis ("Hareesh"), *A Translation of the Vijñāna-bhairava-tantra* (2017, rev. 2018, hareesh.org): "Looking at the clear blue sky, with uninterrupted gaze, remaining completely still... one attains the 'form' of Bhairava."
- **Cross-checked against:** An independently-phrased rendering of the same verse (via secondary sourcing consistent with Jaideva Singh's translation style) renders it as instructing the practitioner, "making himself thoroughly immobile," to behold "the pure (cloudless) sky with fixed eyes" — different wording, same content and same essential instruction (stillness of body, unbroken gaze, cloudless sky), confirming the verse's subject matter across two independent translations.
- **What the verse says:** Look at a clear, cloudless sky. Keep the gaze steady and unmoving, and keep the body still. The verse pairs this with a following verse (85) that adds a visualization of the sky as continuous with the space inside one's own head — an element we have deliberately not carried into the adaptation (see notes below).

## Essence
An unbroken, soft gaze on something with no edges, no texture, and nothing to track gives the visual system nothing to chase — which tends to quiet the small, constant scanning movements the eyes make when looking at anything detailed. The technique isn't about the sky being special; it's about the sky being one of the few genuinely featureless things most people have easy daily access to.

## Guided Session Script
*(Adaptation — modern secular framing. Approximately 4 minutes. Best done outdoors or near a window with a clear or lightly clouded sky visible; an unbroken expanse of blank wall or blank horizon works as a substitute if no sky is visible.)*

Find a spot where you can see a wide stretch of sky without straining your neck — sitting or standing, whichever lets you stay still and comfortable.

Let your gaze settle on the sky, somewhere open and empty rather than on a cloud, a bird, or a plane. Don't stare hard — let your eyes rest there the way they would rest on something plain and unremarkable.

Keep your body still. Let your shoulders drop, your jaw unclench. You're not trying to "see" anything — there's nothing to find. You're just letting your eyes stay in one open space.

If your eyes drift to something with an edge — a rooftop, a tree line, a cloud — gently bring them back to the emptiest part of the sky you can find.

Stay like this for the next few minutes. Notice, without forcing it, whether your visual field starts to feel wider than usual — some people notice their peripheral vision opening up, almost like they're seeing "around" the point they're looking at, not just "at" it.

If your eyes get tired, blink normally — this isn't a stare-without-blinking exercise, just an unhurried, resting gaze.

When you're ready, let your gaze come back down to eye level, take a breath, and carry on with your day.

## Modern Adaptation Notes
- Dropped: the immediately following verse (85), where the sky is visualized as identical with the interior of one's own head and "permeated with the reality of the radiant energy that is Bhairava's nature." This is a specific nondual visualization technique in its own right and would require doctrinal explanation to make sense; we kept verse 84 alone as a self-contained, purely perceptual practice.
- Dropped: "one attains the 'form' of Bhairava" as an outcome claim. Replaced with a plain, falsifiable description of the likely subjective effect (widened peripheral awareness), stated as something some people notice, not a guaranteed transformation.
- Added (adaptation only): a substitute instruction for blank wall/horizon when no sky is visible, and permission to blink normally — the source verse implies unbroken visual fixation without addressing blinking directly; forcing users not to blink is unnecessary and mildly uncomfortable, so we relaxed this without changing the technique's core mechanism.
