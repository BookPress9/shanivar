# Technique 10: Threshold of Sleep

## Primary Source
- **Verse:** Vijñāna-bhairava-tantra, verse 75.
- **Translation cited:** Christopher Wallis ("Hareesh"), *A Translation of the Vijñāna-bhairava-tantra* (2017, rev. 2018, hareesh.org): "When the external sensory field has disappeared but sleep has not yet come, that [liminal] mental state is attainable in which the Supreme Goddess (Parā Devī) manifests."
- **Cross-check status — RESOLVED by QA (2026-09-08):** Independently confirmed against Jaideva Singh's *Vijñānabhairava or Divine Consciousness* (Motilal Banarsidass, 1979; full text via archive.org), Dhāraṇā 52, which is numbered **verse 75** — an exact match to Wallis's numbering. Singh's translation: "When sleep has not yet fully appeared i.e. when one is about to fall asleep, and all the external objects (though present) have faded out of sight, then the state (between sleep and waking) is one on which one should concentrate. In that state the Supreme Goddess will reveal Herself." Two independent scholarly sloka-numbered translations (Wallis and Singh) agree exactly on both verse number and content.
  - **On the "alt. 62" citation:** this research pass traced the source of that alternate number. At least one non-scholarly compilation site explicitly labels this same practice "Technique 62" while itself stating "this is verse 75 in the Sanskrit text" — i.e., "62" there refers to this technique's position in a sequential 1–112 technique count used by some popular guides (following the numbering convention many general-audience compilations use, distinct from the Sanskrit sloka number), not to an alternate scholarly verse numbering. This is a units mismatch (technique-ordinal vs. sloka-number), not a genuine cross-edition sloka discrepancy — no scholarly translation consulted in this pass places this content at sloka 62 (Wallis and Singh both place a different, unrelated practice — the second half of the "gap between two thoughts" technique — at sloka 62; see Technique 09). **Recommendation: standardize on verse 75 (Wallis's and Singh's sloka numbering) and drop the "alt. cited as 62" parenthetical from the technique title/index — it does not reflect a legitimate alternate scholarly edition.**
- **What the verse says:** In the drowsy interval after the outside world's sounds and sights have faded but before sleep has fully arrived, a distinct mental state is available — described in the verse as the appearance of the "Supreme Goddess."

## Essence
This technique doesn't ask the practitioner to create anything; it asks them to notice something that already happens most nights and usually gets missed because it's so brief and because the natural next step is unconsciousness. The hypnagogic state — awake enough to notice, relaxed enough that ordinary thinking has mostly stopped — is treated here as worth a moment of deliberate attention rather than being slept straight through.

## Guided Session Script
*(Adaptation — modern secular framing. Approximately 3–5 minutes, done lying down at bedtime, once you are already drowsy.)*

This one is done lying in bed, lights off, at the point where you're genuinely getting sleepy — not as an alerting exercise, but as something to notice on the way down.

Let your body settle into the bed fully. Don't fight the drowsiness or try to stay sharply alert — that would defeat the point.

As the sounds around you start to fade into the background, and the pull to think in full sentences starts to loosen, notice that shift is happening. You don't need to hold onto it or analyze it — just a light, passing acknowledgment: "this is that in-between place."

If a clear thought or a mental image show up, don't chase it and don't push it away — let it pass through the same way you'd let it pass in a light doze.

There's no goal here beyond noticing you're in that particular state while you're in it, for as long as it naturally lasts before sleep takes over. If you fall asleep before "finishing" — that's not a failure, that's what this state leads to.

If you find yourself becoming fully alert again instead of drowsy, that's fine too — just let go of the exercise for tonight and rest normally.

## Modern Adaptation Notes
- Dropped: "the Supreme Goddess (Parā Devī) manifests" as a specific described outcome. Replaced with a plain, low-key description (a distinct in-between mental state) with no claim about what, if anything, "appears" there.
- Kept: the essential instruction — notice, rather than sleep straight through, the hypnagogic interval between waking sensory awareness and sleep onset.
- Added (adaptation and safety note, not in source): explicit permission to simply fall asleep without "finishing" the exercise, and to abandon it if it causes alertness rather than drowsiness — included because a bedtime practice must not risk interfering with actually falling asleep, which the source text (written for practitioners under direct guru supervision, not as a sleep aid) does not need to address.

Both consulted scholarly translations (Wallis, Singh) place this content at verse 75. Standardize on **verse 75**; flag cleared.

3–5 minutes (at bedtime, self-paced — may end early if sleep arrives)
