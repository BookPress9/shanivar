# Technique 08: Savoring Fully

## Primary Source
- **Verse:** Vijñāna-bhairava-tantra, verse 72.
- **Translation cited:** Christopher Wallis ("Hareesh"), *A Translation of the Vijñāna-bhairava-tantra* (2017, rev. 2018, hareesh.org): "One should meditate on the state of fullness that expands due to the delight of savoring good food and drink; and that joy will become sublime."
- **Cross-checked against:** An independently-phrased rendering of the same verse found via secondary sourcing: "One should meditate on the pleasure that arises from eating and drinking – the bliss of that tasty flavor – and then the state of plenitude and great bliss arise" — different wording, same content, confirming the verse's subject and general placement (part of the text's cluster of verses on savoring ordinary pleasure, verses 65–74).
- **What the verse says:** While eating or drinking something genuinely enjoyable, deliberately meditate on the felt sense of fullness and delight that the taste produces, rather than eating on autopilot.

## Essence
This is one of the more directly usable verses in the text: it doesn't ask the practitioner to imagine anything or adopt a special posture, only to pay full, undistracted attention to a pleasure that is already happening — the taste of food — instead of letting it pass by unnoticed the way eating usually does when it's rushed or paired with a screen.

## Guided Session Script
*(Adaptation — modern secular framing. Approximately 3–4 minutes. Needs a small amount of food or drink you genuinely enjoy — tea, coffee, a piece of fruit, chocolate, or similar.)*

Have your chosen food or drink in front of you. Put away your phone and anything else that would normally have your attention while eating.

Before taking a bite or sip, pause and look at what you're about to taste — really look at it for a few seconds, noticing color, texture, anything you'd normally skip past.

Take a small first bite or sip. Don't swallow right away — let it sit in your mouth for a moment and notice the first wave of flavor as fully as you can.

Swallow, and notice what remains afterward — the aftertaste, the warmth or coolness, whatever lingers.

Take a second bite or sip, slower this time than you normally would. Notice if the flavor is the same as the first taste, or if it's shifted slightly now that you're paying closer attention.

Continue eating or drinking at this slower pace for the next few minutes, treating each bite or sip as the only thing happening right now — no multitasking, no rushing to the next one.

Notice, without needing to name it precisely, whether there's a felt sense of satisfaction or fullness that builds as you continue — not just in your stomach, but as a general quality of the experience.

When you're finished, sit for a breath or two before moving on, rather than immediately jumping to the next task.

## Modern Adaptation Notes
- Dropped: "sublime joy" as a guaranteed outcome. Replaced with an open invitation to notice satisfaction "without needing to name it precisely" — consistent with avoiding overpromising a specific emotional payoff.
- Kept: the verse's core instruction essentially intact — this is one of the least doctrinally loaded verses in the entire text, requiring the least stripping-down of any technique in this set, since it was already describing an ordinary, secular-compatible activity (eating with attention).
- Added (adaptation only): concrete pacing instructions (pause before the first bite, notice the aftertaste, slow the second bite) since the verse itself is a single sentence describing the state to aim for, not a step-by-step method — the steps are our construction, built to reliably produce the attentive-eating state the verse describes.
