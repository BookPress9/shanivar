# Technique 03: Silence After the Hum

## Primary Source
- **Verse:** Vijñāna-bhairava-tantra, verse 39 (with verse 40 as a closely related generalization of the same principle to any sound).
- **Translation cited:** Christopher Wallis ("Hareesh"), *A Translation of the Vijñāna-bhairava-tantra* (2017, rev. 2018, hareesh.org).
- **Cross-checked against:** Wallis's separate 2024 blog post "Vijñāna-bhairava-tantra verses 38 & 39" (hareesh.org), which independently discusses the same verse and confirms it describes the classical Tantrik practice of *uccāra* — a prolonged, whispered or mentally-sustained exhale-syllable (a *praṇava* such as OM) — followed by meditating on the silence that follows once the sound has fully faded.
- **What the verse says:** The practitioner sustains a single-syllable sound for the length of a full exhale, then attends to "the void at the end of its protracted sound" — the silence the sound dissolves into. Verse 40 broadens this: the beginning or ending of *any* syllable, attended to closely enough, opens the same quality of spaciousness.

## Essence
The sound itself is only half the practice; the other half is what's left when the sound stops. This technique uses a long, slow exhaled hum as a way of manufacturing a clean, noticeable silence to land in — the silence is the actual destination, and the hum is just the vehicle that gets you there and then gets out of the way.

## Guided Session Script
*(Adaptation — modern secular framing. Approximately 4 minutes.)*

Sit somewhere you won't feel self-conscious making a low sound out loud. Take a breath in through your nose.

As you exhale, make a soft, low hum — like the sound "mmm" or "ohm," whichever feels more natural in your throat. Let the hum last the entire length of the exhale, fading out gently as your breath runs out rather than stopping it abruptly.

When the hum ends and the breath is empty, don't breathe in right away. Stay for a second or two in the silence that's left behind — notice it specifically, the way you'd notice a room after a loud noise stops.

Breathe in naturally, then repeat: a long, slow hum on the exhale, then a pause to notice the silence after it fades.

Do this for six to eight rounds. Let the hum get quieter each time if that feels right — even a whispered or silent, internal version of the hum on later rounds, if you want to try that.

On the last couple of rounds, skip the hum altogether and just exhale in silence — see if you can find that same quality of "just after a sound has ended," even without a sound to end.

Let your breathing settle back to normal, and sit for a few more seconds before opening your eyes.

## Modern Adaptation Notes
- Dropped: the technical specification of *uccāra* as the internal raising of a *bīja* mantra through the central channel of the subtle body, and the requirement that the syllable be a *praṇava* (a specific class of seed-mantra like OM/AUM with particular ritual weight in the tradition). We kept the structural mechanic — sustained sound, then attend to the silence after — and let the user choose any comfortable low hum rather than assigning religious significance to the specific syllable.
- Dropped: framing the silence as "Spacious Openness... which is the Supreme Power (*parā śakti*)." Replaced with a plain instruction to notice the silence as a specific, describable experience, without a doctrinal label attached to it.
- Combined verses 39 and 40 into one practice (sustained sound → silence, generalized in the closing rounds to any exhale) since they describe the same underlying principle at different levels of specificity; this is noted so QA can verify we are not overclaiming two techniques' worth of citation from what is really one contiguous idea.
