# Technique 09: The Gap Between Two

## Primary Source
- **Verse:** Vijñāna-bhairava-tantra, verse 61 (with verse 62 as a direct continuation of the same instruction).
- **Translation cited:** Christopher Wallis ("Hareesh"), *A Translation of the Vijñāna-bhairava-tantra* (2017, rev. 2018, hareesh.org): "Contemplating the cognition of any two entities (*bhāvas*) or any two states of mind or body (*bhāvas*), take refuge in the center [between them] & rest in the center. Releasing both simultaneously, Reality shines in the Center." Verse 62 continues: after any mental-emotional state has passed, "let awareness be checked: do not move on to another [state right away]."
- **Cross-check status — RESOLVED by QA (2026-09-08):** Independently confirmed against Jaideva Singh's *Vijñānabhairava or Divine Consciousness* (Motilal Banarsidass, 1979; full text via archive.org): Singh's Dhāraṇā 38/39 are numbered **verse 61** and **verse 62** respectively — an exact match to Wallis's numbering. Singh's translation of verse 61: "At the moment when one has perception or knowledge of two objects or ideas, one should simultaneously banish both perceptions or ideas and apprehending the gap or interval between the two, should mentally stick to it... In that gap will Reality flash forth suddenly." Verse 62 continues with the restrained-mind/non-movement-to-another-object instruction, matching Wallis's rendering. The two major scholarly sloka-numbered translations (Wallis and Singh) fully agree on both number and content. The "numbering variance" this research pass previously found in secondary sources is very likely an artifact of some popular non-scholarly compilations (e.g. app/blog summaries of the "112 techniques") numbering practices by **sequential technique-ordinal position** (1st, 2nd, 3rd technique of 112) rather than by **Sanskrit sloka/verse number** — the two counting systems diverge because not every verse contains a distinct technique. This is not a genuine cross-edition sloka-numbering dispute. **Recommendation: standardize on verses 61–62 (Wallis's and Singh's sloka numbering), and do not attempt to reconcile against ordinal "technique #" numbering used by non-scholarly sources — the two systems measure different things and are not meant to match.**
- **What the verse says:** When two distinct thoughts, perceptions, or mental/emotional states arise one after another, direct attention to the interval between them rather than to either state itself, and rest there. Verse 62 adds: after letting a state go, deliberately hold off before picking up the next one.

## Essence
Most attention lives inside thoughts and states, moving from one to the next without noticing the seam between them. This technique treats that seam — the interval where one thing has ended and the next hasn't started — as a legitimate place to rest attention, rather than as a gap to be filled as quickly as possible.

## Guided Session Script
*(Adaptation — modern secular framing. Approximately 5 minutes.)*

Sit comfortably and let your eyes close. Don't try to think about anything in particular — just let your mind do what it normally does.

For the first minute, simply notice thoughts arising and passing, the way you'd notice cars going by. Don't follow any of them, don't push any away — just notice that they come and go.

Now shift the aim slightly: instead of watching the thoughts themselves, try to catch the moment between one thought ending and the next one starting. It will be brief — maybe a fraction of a second at first — but there's a small window there where nothing in particular is happening.

When you catch that window, however briefly, let your attention rest there rather than immediately following whatever thought comes next.

This is subtle and you'll miss it often — that's completely normal. Each time you notice a thought has already started pulling your attention along, that noticing itself is useful; just look for the next gap.

For the next few minutes, keep gently aiming for that in-between space, without frustration if it's hard to find. It's less something you do and more something you notice when your usual habit of chasing the next thought briefly loosens.

In the final minute, drop the effort to find the gap deliberately, and just sit quietly, letting thoughts come and go however they will.

Open your eyes slowly when you're ready.

## Modern Adaptation Notes
- Dropped: "Reality shines in the Center" as a metaphysical claim about the nature of what's found there. Replaced with a plain, low-key description (a window where nothing in particular is happening) that doesn't assert anything beyond what's directly observable.
- Kept: the core instruction essentially intact — attend to the interval between two mental states rather than the states themselves, and (per verse 62) resist rushing into the next state immediately after one ends.
- Added (adaptation only): the opening minute of plain thought-watching, as scaffolding to give a first-time user something concrete to do before attempting the harder task of catching the gap itself — the verse assumes a practitioner who can already observe *bhāvas* clearly, which is a nontrivial skill on its own.

The apparent "variance" traced to earlier secondary sources reflects those sources using an ordinal technique-count rather than the Sanskrit sloka number, not a genuine scholarly-edition disagreement.
