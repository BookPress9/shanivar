# Sadhana roadmap workshop - Phase 2 starting point

Prepared 30 September 2026. Status: **WORKING DRAFT FOR FOUNDER COLLABORATION**. Nothing in this document authorizes native implementation, new practice content or public claims. Routine is Phase 1; this is where we design Sadhana together.

## The product question

Sadhana should make disciplined practice visible and help a person move from needing instruction to practising deliberately in ordinary life. The progression must answer:

> What can this person now do with less prompting, more understanding and more choice?

It should not answer:

> What spiritual level is this person?

Time, session count and confidence can show activity. They cannot prove awareness, realization, nervous-system state or readiness for an intensive method.

## Already decided

- Sadhana is one of two primary destinations beside Routine.
- The practice should increasingly survive without the phone.
- Natural breathing is the current V3 foundation experience, with no pacing, counting or retention.
- A person can pause, end, report no change or discomfort, and return to simpler guidance.
- Traditional sources, modern evidence, product adaptation and personal report remain distinguishable.
- Activity never automatically unlocks intensive breath manipulation, retention, fasting, sleep restriction, trauma excavation, kundalini, sexuality or altered-state techniques.
- Phase 2 can use soft gamification, but it must support real capability and voluntary return rather than status pressure.

## First founder decision: the shape of the path

### Option A - one authored skill path

Everyone moves through the same sequence from foundation to independence. It is easy to understand and measure, but can imply one correct spiritual ladder and fit different users poorly.

### Option B - a library of self-chosen practices

People choose any suitable practice or source. This protects autonomy and tradition-specific depth, but offers little direction and weakens the “next challenge” promise.

### Option C - a stable skill spine with optional paths - recommended

Five capability chapters form a plain shared spine. Within them, a person chooses suitable practices, cultural/source depth and real-life challenges. Advanced means more contextual judgment and independence, not merely more intensity.

This structure can feel progressive without claiming that everyone follows the same inner journey.

## Proposed capability spine

These names are working language, not final ranks.

| Capability | Practical meaning | Example evidence | What it does not prove |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Arrive** | Choose a comfortable anchor and stop automatic momentum briefly | Completes or ends a bounded practice; can identify how to stop/switch | Calmness or meditation ability |
| **Return** | Notice drift and deliberately come back | Uses one chosen cue in a real transition | Constant attention or self-control |
| **See** | Distinguish a present event, interpretation and possible response | Describes a pattern in non-diagnostic language | The hidden cause of a thought/emotion |
| **Choose** | Protect and complete one action that matters | Connects a Routine boundary with an attempted real-world action | Productivity, worth or mental health |
| **Carry** | Practise without the player and decide when guidance is useful | Voluntary self-report of independent practice; reminders reduced | Spiritual attainment or permanent independence |

**Deep study is a branch, not a sixth rank.** It can be opened from the beginning and organized by named tradition/source. An experienced practitioner can use Arrive; a new explorer can read a difficult source.

## Four kinds of evidence

The audit should label every record by how it was produced:

1. **App-observed:** a timer started, paused or ended; a source opened; a Routine boundary was activated.
2. **Device-observed:** only a disclosed, technically verified event such as a protection window being active. Phone behavior is not mind-state evidence.
3. **Self-reported:** a practice or real-world action the person says they attempted.
4. **Reviewed demonstration:** a comprehension or selection task whose criteria were stated in advance. This may establish product understanding, not spiritual readiness.

Missing data stays missing. A person may keep independent practice private and still use the path.

## Soft gamification vocabulary to decide

### Progress surface

- **Path:** clearest, least branded.
- **Orbit:** Shanivar-specific and visually compatible with V3; risk of becoming a cosmetic level system.
- **Cycle:** emphasizes recurrence and return; may be confused with calendar cycles.
- **Practice record:** precise but emotionally flat.

Recommended experiment: compare **Path** and **Orbit** in comprehension interviews. Ask what each implies about success, status and failure before choosing the public term.

### Mechanics worth prototyping

- a visible current capability and next voluntary challenge;
- small challenge cards with purpose, expected effort and completion evidence;
- flexible practice cycles, where a missed day changes nothing and no streak is lost;
- “practised without Shanivar” as a meaningful milestone;
- a quiet recap showing attempts, discomfort and independent reports without collapsing them into one score;
- chapter completion based on varied evidence, not timer accumulation alone;
- explicit return to an earlier practice without losing standing;
- fewer reminders as progress.

### Mechanics to exclude from the first prototype

- XP detached from a demonstrated skill;
- leaderboards or public comparison;
- streak loss, freezes, paid recovery or shame copy;
- spiritual titles, consciousness levels or readiness scores;
- hidden recommendation criteria;
- rewards for longer breath holds, greater intensity, altered states or ignoring discomfort;
- source access locked behind activity.

## Challenge grammar

Every proposed challenge needs:

```text
Moment        When might this help?
Capability    What ordinary skill is being practised?
Action        What is the smallest clear instruction?
Choice        How can the person change, skip or stop?
Evidence      What is observed, self-reported or left unknown?
Boundary      What must not be inferred?
Return        What ordinary action follows?
```

Example working card:

```text
Moment: Before opening a familiar feed
Capability: Return
Action: Notice one natural breath, then name what you meant to do
Choice: Skip, use an external cue, or open the app if it is genuinely needed
Evidence: Optional self-report that a chosen action was attempted
Boundary: Does not diagnose compulsion or prove attention control
Return: Continue the chosen task or deliberately use the app
```

## Content lanes

The requested source collections should enter Sadhana through labelled lanes rather than becoming one ladder.

| Lane | Initial scope | Progress role |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Attention practice** | Existing reviewed Steady material and low-intensity foundation cues | Core skill practice |
| **Manovigyana inquiry** | Non-diagnostic observation, interpretation, action and agency questions | See and Choose challenges |
| **Daily physiology context** | Sleep opportunity, daylight, movement and social connection from appropriately reviewed sources | Routine-linked context, not clinical optimization |
| **Tradition study** | Named VBT, Yoga, Buddhist, Vedanta and other source records kept separate | Optional depth available at any time |
| **Independent application** | Practice outside the app, chosen focus, conversation, creative or service action | Carry evidence |
| **Qualified/research-only** | Intensive breath, retention, fasting, sleep restriction, strong somatic, kundalini, sexuality, altered states | Context only; never activity-unlocked |

The Huberman protocol mind map can help identify topics, but every card needs a primary/authoritative check and exact claim review. The JKP mental-health PDF contributes a useful `concept -> example -> apply` teaching pattern; it does not authorize diagnostic exercises or inference from app behavior.

## Proposed first Sadhana MVP

Keep the first test smaller than the full roadmap:

1. Foundation Steady session.
2. One **Return** challenge tied to an unwanted feed-opening moment.
3. One **Choose** challenge tied to a user-selected Routine block.
4. “I practised without Shanivar” optional record.
5. A factual audit with app-observed and self-reported labels.
6. Path/Orbit progress prototype with no public rank and no streak.
7. One source-and-adaptation view.

This is enough to test whether progression helps practice transfer. It does not require a large content library or advanced methods.

## Proposed evaluation

Test with newcomers, returning meditators and experienced practitioners. Include people who dislike streaks or body-focused practice.

Primary question: can participants explain what changed in their capability and use it outside the app?

Supporting observations:

- time to choose a suitable next challenge;
- correct understanding of observed versus self-reported evidence;
- voluntary off-app practice;
- ability to move backward or stop without shame;
- reduction in reminders chosen over time;
- confusion about rank, readiness or mental-health claims;
- discomfort, pressure or compulsive completion behavior.

Failure includes a path that makes people optimize the record, fear missing days, equate completion with inner worth, attempt unsuitable practices or spend more time navigating than practising.
