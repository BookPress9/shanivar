# Psychology and practice-safety source ledger

Research date: 2026-09-29. Researcher role: Manovigyana / Human Psychology, invoked after the Staff Manager audit. All product implications are PROPOSED. These are primary research reports, author-written theoretical syntheses, or official health guidance; none establishes the truth of a metaphysical tradition. No licensed book was copied or represented as fully read.

Access vocabulary: **sections** means inspected relevant methods/results/discussion within an available full article, not exhaustive reanalysis; **abstract** means author abstract inspected; **indexed excerpts** means official-source search extracts available but opening failed. No raw data were reanalysed. Sources cited by a paper were not thereby reviewed. Numerical claims below are confined to the inspected source. Study-specific populations and definitions limit transfer to Shanivar.

| ID | Bibliography / direct source | Access and design | What it supports; limits |
|---|---|---|---|
| P01 | Watkins (2008), [Constructive and unconstructive repetitive thought](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18298268/), Psychological Bulletin, DOI 10.1037/0033-2909.134.2.163 | Author abstract; theoretical evidence review, not a new trial | Repetitive thought can help or hinder depending on content, context and processing. Not evidence that all thinking should stop or a one-line prompt treats rumination. |
| P02 | Grüning, Riedel & Lorenz-Spreen (2023), [Directing smartphone use through the self-nudge app one sec](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36795756/), PNAS, DOI 10.1073/pnas.2213114120 | Abstract; six-week field sample N=280 plus preregistered online dismantling N=500; PMC opening blocked | Field change does not isolate cause. Online experiment favored a dismissal choice and delay; deliberation message ineffective. App developer is an author. Laboratory video consumption is not long-term agency. |
| P03 | Castelo et al. (2025), [Blocking mobile internet on smartphones improves sustained attention, mental health, and subjective well-being](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39967678/), PNAS Nexus, DOI 10.1093/pnasnexus/pgaf017 | Abstract + indexed publisher methods excerpt; preregistered delayed-intervention RCT N=467, two-week whole-internet block | Calls/texts and nonphone internet remained possible. Broad restriction differs from Shanivar's selected apps. Compliance/attrition and expectancy constrain interpretation; do not export effect sizes or “brain age” claims. Full compliance analysis needs deeper inspection. |
| P04 | Mehling et al. (2012), [The Multidimensional Assessment of Interoceptive Awareness](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0048230), PLOS ONE | Methods/results/limitations sections; instrument-development field sample N=325, largely experienced mind-body practitioners | Body awareness has separable reported dimensions; one-factor score fit poorly. Sample mostly female, white, educated. Questionnaire is not objective sensation accuracy, diagnosis or consciousness measurement. |
| P05 | Chin et al. (2019), [Psychological mechanisms driving stress resilience in mindfulness training](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6681655/), Health Psychology, DOI 10.1037/hea0000763 | Methods/results sections; preregistered RCT N=137, eight-week Monitor+Accept vs Monitor Only vs no treatment | Acceptance-inclusive training improved daily stress reports relative to alternatives. Screened English-speaking adults; instructor/course bundle and self-report. Cannot generalize the effect to a ten-second app instruction. |
| P06 | Balban et al. (2023), [Brief structured respiration practices enhance mood and reduce physiological arousal](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9873947/), Cell Reports Medicine, DOI 10.1016/j.xcrm.2022.100895 | Methods/results sections; remote four-arm trial N=108, 28 days; retrospectively registered; 24 meditation / 84 across breathing arms | Positive-affect and respiration findings favor cyclic sighing; no significant HRV, resting-heart-rate or sleep changes. Small uneven groups, selected largely student sample, excluded substantial medical/psychiatric conditions. Does not validate VBT or universal breathwork. |
| P07 | Fincham et al. (2023), [Effect of coherent breathing on mental health and wellbeing](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-023-49279-8), Scientific Reports, DOI 10.1038/s41598-023-49279-8 | Indexed official methods/discussion excerpts; opening failed; randomized active-control study N=400 | Slow coherent pacing did not outperform matched faster pacing on primary stress or secondary outcomes. Both conditions' improvement cannot prove specific rhythm efficacy. Not proof every breathing practice is ineffective. |
| P08 | Farias et al. (2020), [Adverse events in meditation practices and meditation-based therapies](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32820538/), Acta Psychiatrica Scandinavica, DOI 10.1111/acps.13225 | Abstract; systematic review, 83 studies / 6,703 participants | Adverse experiences are documented; definitions/designs vary sharply. Pooled 8.3% is not a Shanivar incidence estimate. Retrospective, case and experimental evidence should not be combined into individual risk prediction. |
| P09 | Lindahl et al. (2017), [The varieties of contemplative experience](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0176239), PLOS ONE | Sampling/results/limitations sections; purposive mixed-methods, 60 practitioners and 32 experts (11 overlap) | Taxonomy of challenges and interpretive variation, not population prevalence. Selected Western Buddhist practitioners with difficulties, predominantly white/educated; intensive practice common. Neither every challenge nor every unusual experience establishes illness or spiritual progress. |
| P10 | Altendorf et al. (2019), [Should or could?](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30834136/), Digital Health, DOI 10.1177/2055207619832767 | Abstract; 2×2 language/choice experiment N=521 | No significant framing effect on autonomy support/reactance in tailored alcohol communication. Important null comparator to simple claims that replacing “must” with “could” reliably changes behavior. |
| P11 | Psychological Science Accelerator SDT Collaboration (2022), [A global experiment on motivating social distancing](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35622891/), PNAS, DOI 10.1073/pnas.2111091119 | Abstract; preregistered experiment N=25,718, 89 countries | Controlling language increased controlled motivation; autonomy framing reduced defiance relative to controlling text. No effect on behavioral intentions/autonomous motivation. Public-health intentions are not actual app adherence. |
| P12 | Lally et al. (2010), [How are habits formed](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/ejsp.674), EJSP | Bibliographic publisher record + indexed article abstract; longitudinal N=96, 12 weeks, self-selected daily actions | Repetition in a stable context and modeled automaticity vary. No universal 21/66-day promise; behavior and self-report are not spiritual attainment. Full modeling not independently reviewed here. |
| P13 | Gruber, Gelman & Ranganath (2014), [States of curiosity modulate hippocampus-dependent learning](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4252494/), Neuron, DOI 10.1016/j.neuron.2014.08.060 | Methods/results sections; trivia-memory/fMRI and delayed behavioral experiments. Reported t-test degrees of freedom imply analysis Ns of 19/18 and 28/27 respectively; enrollment/exclusions in supplement remain inaccessible | Curiosity-related memory differences in laboratory tasks. Brain activity does not prove “dopamine reset” or that mystery hooks improve contemplative practice. Analysis Ns are inferred from reported paired t tests, not verified enrollment counts. |
| P14 | Monsell, Yeung & Azuma (2000), [Reconfiguration of task-set](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11004879/), Psychological Research | Abstract excerpt; task-switching experiments; N not extracted | Switching can slow responding/increase errors; task structure matters. No direct estimate of everyday productivity loss or justification for treating every interruption as harmful. |
| P15 | Roemer & Borkovec (1994), [Effects of suppressing thoughts about emotional material](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7930046/), Journal of Abnormal Psychology, DOI 10.1037/0021-843x.103.3.467 | Author abstract; suppression/expression experiment; N not extracted | Suppression-related rebound in the reported task. Use as a bounded mechanism lead; do not make therapeutic claims or prescribe exposure. |
| P16 | Saults & Cowan (2007), [A central capacity limit to simultaneous storage of visual and auditory arrays](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17999578/), Journal of Experimental Psychology: General, DOI 10.1037/0096-3445.136.4.663 | Indexed full methods excerpts for experiments 1–2: 28 recruited / 24 analyzed and 24 recruited / 23 analyzed, undergraduate samples. Five experiments total; remaining samples not extracted | Limited capacity is a research context, not a law that every screen must have an exact number of options. Design rule remains a testable heuristic. |
| P17 | Waters et al. (2018), [Severe sleep deprivation causes hallucinations…](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30042701/), Frontiers in Psychiatry, DOI 10.3389/fpsyt.2018.00303 | Abstract; systematic review, 21 studies / 760 people, heterogeneous prolonged deprivation | Supports exclusion of sleep-deprivation challenges. Historical extreme protocols do not estimate risk of ordinary late bedtimes; do not extract a supposedly safe wakefulness dose. |
| P18 | NCCIH, [Meditation and mindfulness: effectiveness and safety](https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/meditation-and-mindfulness-effectiveness-and-safety) | Current official guidance sections | Harms understudied; care should not be replaced/delayed. Guidance is not an app-specific safety certification. |
| P19 | NCCIH, [Yoga: effectiveness and safety](https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/yoga-effectiveness-and-safety) | Current official risks/guidance sections | Beginners should avoid forceful breathing/extreme practices; conditions and accessibility require modification. Broad yoga umbrella does not establish every tantric technique's safety. |
| P20 | NIDDK (2020), [Fasting safely with diabetes](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/professionals/diabetes-discoveries-practice/fasting-safely-with-diabetes) | Official clinician interview, risk sections | Fasting can interact with illness/medication and hydration. Supports no generic app fasting prescription; this document gives no medication advice. |
| P21 | Lindholm & Gennser (2005), [Aggravated hypoxia during breath-holds after prolonged exercise](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15778900/) | Author abstract excerpt; physiological experiment with eight experienced breath-hold divers | Hyperventilation/breath-hold aquatic risk is context-specific and serious. Supports excluding holds and unsafe settings, not diagnosing ordinary breathing difficulty. |
| P22 | Zaccari et al. (2023), [Yoga vs cognitive processing therapy for military sexual trauma–related PTSD](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamanetworkopen/fullarticle/2812725), JAMA Network Open, DOI 10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2023.44862 | Abstract and publisher sample/CONSORT sections; multisite clinical RCT N=131 (72 yoga, 59 CPT), substantial postrandomization attrition | Trauma-sensitive interventions can be studied in care, but that evidence cannot establish an unattended body prompt as trauma treatment. Clinical trial and app script are different interventions. |

P14/P15 have no sample-size extraction; P13 enrollment details and P16 experiments 3–5 remain incompletely inspected. They support bounded qualitative claims only. P07 publisher/PMC excerpts were inspected but not represented as a full-text read; repeated direct opening failed in this researcher session. These limitations are actionable research tasks, not evidence of absence.
