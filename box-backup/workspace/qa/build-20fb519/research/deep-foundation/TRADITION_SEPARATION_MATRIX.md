# Tradition separation matrix

Status: **PROPOSED**, 29 September 2026. This matrix is a compression aid, not a replacement for texts or a ranking of traditions. Source IDs resolve to [the ledger](SOURCE_LEDGER_ANCIENT.md), including date, edition, access depth and uncertainty. Interpretations below are source-attributed summaries; product rules are original synthesis.

## Similar vocabulary can conceal different theories

| Term | Difference that must remain visible | UI/research consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Manas / citta / vijñāna | Their scope and place differ across systems | Store original term, tradition and local definition; never one global “mind” ontology |
| Ahaṃkāra / asmitā / anattā | I-making, a Yoga affliction and not-self discourse are different concepts | “Ego score” is rejected; ordinary self-reference is not a defect |
| Saṃskāra | Context may involve dispositions, conditioning or other technical meanings | “Habit” may be an analogy; it is not a complete translation |
| Śūnya / śūnyatā | Meaning depends on text and philosophical commitments | Never merge VBT spatial contemplation with Madhyamaka doctrine solely because both use emptiness language |
| Prāṇa / vāyu | Breath can be involved without exhausting the concept | No conversion into oxygen level, electrical charge or vagal tone |
| Dhyāna / jhāna | Specific usages and training contexts differ | A one-minute session is not automatically the named attainment |
| Tantra / yoga | Textual genre, tradition, discipline and methods overlap historically but are not synonyms | User entry stays concrete; optional origins supply the correct scope |
| Guru / teacher | Traditional authority/relationship is not equivalent to software personalization | Architectural metaphor only; app can be wrong and must accept disagreement |

The glossary is an **editorial caution**, not a new dictionary. Before a technical term enters public teaching, attach a passage and a translation-specific explanation from the ledger. Compare the same term within its context before comparing traditions.

## Where comparison is useful

There is room for a practical comparison of trained attention, repeated return, ethical consequences and investigation of habitual identification. Similarity at this level does not show a common historical origin, an identical technique, or a scientifically verified mechanism. Borrowing and disagreement can coexist. This distinction is supported by the cross-tradition comparisons in A01–11; the product conclusion is Shanivar's.

Proposed test: after a shared 20-second observation, show two short source-labelled interpretations only to users who request them. Success means users can say what they directly observed and what each tradition adds. Failure means they feel pressured to endorse a doctrine or believe the app has settled the debate. Decision: **test**, with no spiritual status score and a clear “Leave this interpretation aside” exit.

Editorial acceptance check: a reviewer must be able to answer which text, which edition, which commentary, which claim, and which app modification. Any answer of “ancient wisdom says” fails provenance review.
