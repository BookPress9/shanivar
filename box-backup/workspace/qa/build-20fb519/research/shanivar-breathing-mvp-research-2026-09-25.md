# Shanivar breathing MVP: Coherence, competitors, books and product direction

Research date: 25 September 2026. Status: research and proposed MVP, not an implemented or clinically validated feature.

## Recommendation and founder direction

Build a small breathing experience around **Pause, Prepare and Unwind**, connected to Shanivar's alarms and focus protection. The product promise is: **Start your day. Protect your attention. Take a breath and choose what comes next.**

The founder confirmed the reference app is [Coherence](https://www.coherencebreath.com/) and described the audience as everyone who wants to prevent “brain rot”: action-oriented alarms get people out of bed, app locks preserve focus, and breathing supports them. This broadens the audience framing in older repository documents. Family introductions remain a possible acquisition route, not the definition of the audience.

Treat “brain rot” as audience language for unwanted scrolling, fragmented attention and difficulty beginning meaningful activities. It is not a diagnosis or a measurable physiological outcome. Test whether the phrase attracts younger people while alienating others. Public copy should describe the behavior Shanivar helps with.

The strongest candidate advantage is the connection between **waking, protecting time, noticing an urge, breathing briefly and returning to a chosen action**. This is a product hypothesis, not established market whitespace. In particular, one sec already combines breathing with interruption of app opening.

## Research scope and limits

Reviewed official product websites, app-store descriptions and release notes, author/publisher resources, accessible book excerpts and research papers. Product descriptions establish advertised offerings, not hands-on reliability. No paid accounts were created and no apps were installed. Prices are public snapshots, not verified checkout quotes. “Top competitors” below means strategically relevant alternatives, not a measured market-share ranking.

Book coverage is explicitly partial: selected accessible text was read; several titles were assessed through publisher descriptions or author teaching material. No claim is made to have read all these books cover to cover. Full-text review of the selected core books remains a content-development dependency before adapting detailed programs. This brief contains original synthesis, not copied scripts.

## Coherence: offering and assessment

### Confirmed reference product

Coherence positions itself around choosing calm, focus, energy or sleep. Its website emphasizes animated guidance, eyes-closed audio and globally synchronized practice. Named practices include Coherence, Vortex, Tesla, Samadhi, Aura and Sri Yantra. These names mix conventional breathing ideas with branded and spiritual presentation. The site displays numerical claims about cognition, cortisol and oxygen utilization, with broad institutional/journal attribution; the reviewed page does not establish study-to-protocol support for those percentages. Do not reuse them as evidence for Shanivar. [Official website](https://www.coherencebreath.com/).

The iOS listing advertises 500+ patterns, a sequence editor, recorded voice guidance, music links, offline/screen-off practice, history, streaks, leagues and leaderboards. Release notes describe global chat, health-data integrations and a Regulation Index. These are advertised capabilities; importing HRV is not itself validation of a regulation score. Its description quotes $9.99 monthly/$39.99 annual, but purchase entries show $14.99 monthly and $69.99 membership. The latter membership's period is not explicit. Pricing is inconsistent and requires checkout verification. [US App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/coherence-breathwork/id6740344645).

The India listing shows ₹1,499 for monthly membership and ₹6,900 for several membership entries; do not infer the duration of ambiguously named entries. It lists English as the app language. This provides a reason to investigate affordable Hindi/English delivery, not proof that localization alone will win. [India App Store](https://apps.apple.com/in/app/coherence-breathwork/id6740344645).

Android is listed under Coherence World Wide. The store advertises a three-day trial on the annual plan. Visible complaints include timing settings, freezing and audio/background issues. These are anecdotal reports, not measured defect prevalence or proof that current versions retain those defects. They are useful prompts for Shanivar's device QA. [Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?hl=en&id=com.coherenceapp.breath).

### Product interpretation

Coherence combines a breathing tool, creator library and social practice network. Competing on the number of techniques would require considerable content, moderation and operational work. Shanivar should prioritize a reliable, fast route into a useful session and back to life. Its value must survive a comparison with a free breathing timer.

Adapt the usability principles of clear phase guidance, optional sound, screen-off operation and few actions before starting. Create original visuals and audio. Defer global synchronization, public profiles, competitive minutes, user-generated protocols and composite physiology scores. These do not test Shanivar's immediate behavioral promise.

There are unrelated similarly named apps: Genux's [Cardiac Coherence Breathing](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?hl=en&id=com.genux.coherence) advertises free offline practice without an account; [Coherence by Presage](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.presagetech.app) advertises camera-based vital-sign estimation. Their features must not be attributed to the founder's reference app.

## What the evidence supports

| Evidence reviewed | Finding and limitation | Design consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fincham et al., 2023 meta-analysis | Stress analysis: 12 randomized trials, 785 adults; small-to-medium average benefit, Hedges' g about −0.35. Most studies had moderate risk of bias. Heterogeneous interventions cannot validate every named pattern. | Describe breathing as something that may help people settle, not a guaranteed reset. [Paper](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-022-27247-y) |
| Balban et al., 2023 randomized study | 108 enrolled participants; five minutes daily for 28 days. Cyclic sighing showed advantages over mindfulness meditation for positive affect and respiratory-rate change. No significant improvements in the measured sleep outcomes; no basis for claiming universal superiority or that one sigh reproduces the study. | A useful candidate for later guided testing. Do not use this study to promise better sleep or instant results. [Full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9873947/) |
| Fincham et al., 2023 active-control trial | 400 participants; approximately ten minutes daily for four weeks. Coherent breathing around 5.5 breaths/minute did not outperform a matched 12-breaths/minute attention control on mental health and wellbeing. | Test guided breathing against a matched pause. Avoid claiming a uniquely effective universal rhythm. [Paper](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-023-49279-8) |
| Vetter et al., 2026 ASIP study | Series of four-week N-of-1 trials in 76 resident physicians, assessing mindfulness breathing or box breathing. Findings do not support a blanket prescription; individual responses matter. This narrow occupational sample does not represent everyone. | Make “no change” and “felt worse” legitimate feedback. Keep methods optional. [Paper](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41746-025-02317-3) |
| one sec research | Evaluates an app intervention combining friction, deliberation and an option to abandon opening. | Evidence for a combined intervention cannot isolate breathing as the cause, or establish Shanivar's effect. [Paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9974409/) |

Important distinction: 5.5 seconds in plus 5.5 seconds out equals approximately 5.45 breaths/minute. It is not the same specification as exactly 5.5 breaths/minute. A chosen pacing rhythm is also not a measured personal resonance frequency.

The useful synthesis is that there are several different tasks: attending to natural breathing, gently pacing it, training performance and undertaking traditional contemplative practice. They should not be bundled into one escalating difficulty ladder.

## Breathing for different set and setting

Here “set” means current state, intention, experience and comfort; “setting” means place, posture, available time and whether someone must stay alert. The rows below are proposed product choices, not validated prescriptions. Specific timing and scripts need qualified review and usability testing.

| Moment / set | Setting | Proposed experience | Scope and evidence boundary |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Automatic reach for a distracting app** | Stationary, able to look away safely; 30–90 seconds | Pause: observe natural breathing or use a comfortable gentle pacer, then choose the next action. | MVP. Duration is a friction/usability hypothesis, not a proven physiological dose. |
| **Ready to begin work or study** | Seated at desk; 1–3 minutes | Prepare: unforced equal in/out pacing if comfortable, then begin the selected focus session. | MVP. Assess task initiation; do not promise improved intelligence or attention treatment. |
| **Wired before bed** | Safely seated or lying down; 3–5 minutes | Unwind: gentle pacing with optional slightly longer exhale, fading screen and quiet ending. | MVP. Support winding down; no guaranteed sleep onset or apnea treatment. |
| **Just completed the wake-up action** | Stop moving and settle first | Offer a brief optional natural-breath pause, then show the chosen morning action. | Integration point. Never require breath holds or a breathing score to dismiss an alarm. |
| **Before a difficult conversation/exam** | Private or discreet seated pause; 1–3 minutes | Reuse Prepare; choose what to say/do next. | Test as a context, not a new technique. No guarantee of calm or performance. |
| **Panic-prone or uncomfortable with internal sensations** | Safe stationary place, eyes open if preferred | Offer an external-attention pause or exit instead of controlling the breath. | Never force deep breaths, slow counts or closed eyes. Not a panic-treatment feature. |
| **Quiet meditation or prayer** | Chosen quiet setting; user-controlled duration | Natural breath observation; optional respectful contemplative framing. | Later context. Distinguish spiritual meaning from scientific evidence. |
| **Exercise, athletic conditioning or singing** | Task-specific training setting | Dedicated instruction suited to the activity. | Defer. Resting slow-pacing ratios should not become a universal exertion prescription. |
| **Driving, cycling, swimming, bath/shower or hazardous work** | Requires alertness or presents consequences of fainting/distraction | Do not start an immersive guided session; invite a pause later in a safe place. | Product restriction, especially important for any future retention/intensive practices. |

NHS guidance supports comfortable, unforced breathing and building a regular practice. Breath-Body-Mind's public guidance permits eyes-open practice and gentler breathing rather than forcing the lungs full. Wim Hof's own safety guidance excludes its intensive breathing from water and driving contexts. These support conservative design; they do not establish one protocol for every user. [NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/mental-health/self-help/guides-tools-and-activities/breathing-exercises-for-stress/), [BBM FAQ](https://www.breathbodymindfoundation.org/bbm-resources/faqs-coherent-breathing), [Wim Hof safety guidance](https://www.wimhofmethod.com/practice-the-method).

## Concrete MVP proposal

### Three entries, one small practice engine

**Pause:** “Make room before your next action.” Default 60 seconds, natural-breath option immediately available.

**Prepare:** “Take a moment, then begin.” Default two minutes; offer comfortable equal pacing. A candidate preset is four seconds in/four seconds out, with five/five available if comfortable. These are prototype settings, not individualized resonance prescriptions.

**Unwind:** “Give the day a quiet ending.” Default three minutes; candidate four-in/six-out pacing only if comfortable, with equal pacing or natural breathing available. Never require finishing a timed phase when it feels uncomfortable.

Minimum journey: choose a moment → check comfort and duration → start → stop/end → optionally note “more settled / same / less settled” → choose or resume the next action. Unwind should end quietly rather than demand a bright-screen questionnaire. Feedback can be deferred or skipped.

Shared functionality:

- Original visual pacer, optional simple sound and haptics, reduced motion, readable text and screen-reader labels.
- Offline core sessions; no account needed to try a breathing session.
- Reliable screen-off operation and clear behavior during calls, audio interruptions and app backgrounding.
- Immediate stop control, comfortable natural-breath alternative and no breath holds in the initial library.
- Fixed reviewed content with duration and comfort choices. No LLM-generated breathing prescriptions.
- English/Hindi delivery as a testable India priority; review spoken wording with native speakers and include older-adult usability sessions.
- Minimal voluntary feedback, local history where practical and an optional cue to practice without the app.

Connection to existing features: after an alarm's required action is completed and its audio is silenced, offer Prepare; before Focus, offer a short preparation; during an urge, offer Pause without misrepresenting or silently changing the app-lock state. A breathing session must not become another mandatory barrier. “Lock any app” remains an ambition: actual supported selections, permissions and bypass limits need accurate product wording.

Do not add rapid ventilation, long holds, maximal inhalation contests, 4-7-8 as a required beginner default, mouth taping, camera diagnostics, HRV scores, leagues, social feeds or hundreds of techniques to this MVP. Each would introduce a separate validation or operational burden without answering the core product question.

### Comfort and content quality

Suggested original instruction: “Let your breath stay easy. Follow the rhythm only while it feels comfortable. You can return to your normal breathing or stop at any time.”

Stop guidance should cover dizziness, air hunger or increasing distress; do not describe these as proof of healing or encourage pushing through. Persistent or severe symptoms belong outside an app exercise. Qualified review should consider respiratory/cardiovascular conditions, pregnancy and users distressed by breath attention before public release. Broad audience ambition is not evidence of suitability for all ages or conditions; start evaluation with adults, then design and verify other cohorts explicitly.

Content should never claim a dopamine reset, brain repair, cure for anxiety, objectively measured regulation without sensing, or physiological proof of spiritual beliefs. A session ending does not prove the user followed the rhythm or achieved an outcome.

### Validate usefulness and commercial value

First run 8–12 observed sessions across younger adults and older adults, including Hindi/English users. Resolve confusion, comfort and device reliability before retention testing. Then run a proposed 2–4 week feasibility pilot of roughly 30–50 adults who recognize a specific attention problem. These are planning choices, not a powered clinical trial.

Compare an optional brief breathing session with a similarly timed ordinary pause before the same chosen action. Randomize or counterbalance where feasible; preserve the option to stop or skip. This tests whether guidance adds value beyond simply interrupting the loop.

Measure voluntary repeat use, comfort, return to the chosen task, and willingness to use/pay for the combined product. Separate a Focus-start event from evidence of actually doing the intended task. Where available and explicitly permitted, combine task-start timing with a short later self-report. Report results by context and age/language cohort, plus missing responses and withdrawals.

Proposed feasibility gates: most observed users can start and stop without help; no unresolved serious safety issue; at least half of pilot participants voluntarily return on three separate days in week two; a majority of responding sessions are described as helpful for the next action, with response rate and discomfort reported alongside. These thresholds are founder-adjustable heuristics, not clinical standards or proof of market fit. If a plain pause performs similarly, retain the simpler experience and make breathing a preference.

Keep basic breathing free during validation. Test willingness to pay for the combined alarm/focus/breathing value before proposing a standalone breathing subscription. Coherence's public pricing inconsistencies and free competitors make copying a price unjustified. Possible India price experiments should be labeled experiments and tested through concrete offers, not inferred from enthusiastic interviews.

### Implementation acceptance criteria for a later build

- Session timing remains coherent across screen lock, pause/resume and background transitions; no catch-up burst of cues after interruption.
- Stop always works and ends audio; interrupted sessions are not recorded as completed.
- Breathing never delays alarm dismissal or silently disables focus protection.
- No network dependency for the core session, and no unexpected camera/microphone/health permissions.
- Text scaling, reduced motion, silent use and eyes-open use remain workable.
- Every technique has reviewed instructions, context, exclusions, source links and a version identifier.
- Analytics distinguish offered, started, stopped, completed and skipped sessions without claiming to measure actual breathing.

## Outstanding research before launch

1. Inspect Coherence and the closest alternatives on real Android/iOS devices, including Indian paywalls, offline behavior and audio interruptions.
2. Obtain legitimate full copies of the prioritized books and review exact passages before authoring a larger curriculum; commission qualified review of the initial scripts.
3. Test the three proposed moments against an ordinary pause. Do not assume feeling calmer produces better attention or action.
4. Validate language, age-related accessibility, willingness to pay and safe population boundaries. Everyone is the long-term audience; evidence must be built cohort by cohort.

This research supports breathing as a candidate third pillar of Shanivar. The next decision is whether a small, comfortable breathing experience measurably helps users begin or return to what they chose to do.
