# Three public concepts for review — GROK-001

4 October 2026. Concepts only. Not captions, not a calendar, not testimonials, not released app content, and not a claim that Shanivar is publicly available. Grok is not a sadhak and has no bodily or spiritual experience to offer. No person is quoted. Illustrative scenes are fictional situations, not customers.

These are proposals for the Social Media Manager to accept, narrow, or reject under `marketing/shanivar/marketing-operating-system.md`. They do not accept ST-001–ST-008. Decision 0014 (Routine / Sadhana product form) is a different decision and is not what these concepts advertise.

Shared rules for all three:

- The viewer can do the glance, do nothing, change the anchor, or stop. No-change is a finished outcome.
- No permanent bliss, aura or chakra diagnosis, forced breathwork, secret-knowledge bait, or medical claim.
- Daoist qi, Indian kundalini/chakras, Tibetan inner fire, and empirical physiology are not used as explanations.
- Vedic astrology is not used. If it ever appears elsewhere, it is an interpretive lens, not a prediction. It is not needed here.
- Personality to aim at, from recorded founder intention: a patient teacher. The viewer keeps authority. The concept does not say Shanivar knows the viewer.

## 1. The shade that is allowed to mean nothing

**Lived opening.** Someone is halfway through a scroll. The brightest color in the room is the phone. They look up. A cup, a sleeve, or the wall is already there, in an ordinary color, in ordinary light.

**Exact proposition.** For one glance, a person may notice which ordinary color they linger on, and what association shows up, if any. In the Palmer and Schloss work as recorded in SI-10, object associations help explain color preferences in that study. That finding is not this exercise, and it is not a reading of the person. “Nothing comes to mind” is a complete answer.

**Excluded claims.** Not an aura, chakra, health, personality, or rank diagnosis. Not Clement’s color-healing sequence, not JobCannon’s seven-color map, not phototherapy, not alpha waves, not a meditation treatment. Not “the masters hid the real meaning of colors.” Not proof that Shanivar changes behavior. Do not put the PNAS paper on screen as validation of the glance. The ledger’s access to that paper was abstract plus discussion of the theory on 2 October 2026, and this pass did not reread it.

**Format.** One 20–30 second vertical concept, or a four-frame silent carousel with the same beats. Future manager brief only. AI scenes, if ever made, stay illustrative and disclosed. Not UGC testimony.

**20–30 second outline.**

1. 0–5s. Phone light. Look up. One ordinary object in the room.
2. 5–12s. “Which shade do you linger on? A cup, a sleeve, the wall.”
3. 12–20s. “If it reminds you of something, name that quietly. If it reminds you of nothing, that counts.”
4. 20–28s. “You can stop. You do not have to breathe differently, and you do not have to feel anything. Then go back to what you were doing.”

**Visual idea.** Daylight, no glow, no body outline, no rainbow stacked on a spine. Two frames of real objects side by side, then a frame with no color highlight at all, so “nothing” has a picture. On-screen words are short enough to read with the sound off. No Devanagari claim is made here because none was verified for this concept.

**Why it fits the stance.** Recorded founder intention is to notice automatic loops and leave authority with the person. The README’s public hook (“Which color draws you in today—and what does it remind you of?”) is the neighbor of this concept; this version adds an explicit nothing-outcome so the glance cannot be heard as a test the viewer can fail. ST-002 and ST-004 remain PROPOSED; the concept borrows their caution (optional, stop, no mandatory breath) without calling them accepted doctrine. It does not implement decision 0014 and must not be narrated as if the app already teaches this.

## 2. Contact before the reply

**Lived opening.** A message is waiting. The hand is already on the table, or already around a cup. The reply has not been typed.

**Exact proposition.** Before reacting, attention can rest for a few seconds on a contact that is already happening: weight, temperature, or texture. Breathing stays as it is. Eyes can stay open. If the contact is unpleasant, the person looks at something outside the body or stops. No sensation is required. This is an original attention proposal. Not found in the local corpus: any study that tested “hand on a cup for a few seconds” as an intervention, or any result that this reduces reactivity.

**Excluded claims.** Not qi, prana, kundalini, nadis, chakras, tummo, winds, or drops. Not a nervous-system score and not a use of the PLOS ONE perfusion findings (those belong to high-ventilation breathwork in experienced, screened participants, with discomfort noted, and are not a beginner method). Not forced breath, retention, or locks. Not trauma release, anxiety treatment, or insomnia treatment. Not a claim that the person was “not in their body” and has now been fixed. Not a Shanivar alarm or focus-mode demo, because release evidence for public availability was not read and the marketing operating system still says Steady is in development for the current batch.

**Format.** One 20–30 second vertical concept. No guided-breathing audio. Silent version must carry the same meaning.

**20–30 second outline.**

1. 0–6s. Thumb hovering over a reply. No send.
2. 6–14s. “Feel the weight already in your hand, or look at the table if you would rather not go inward.”
3. 14–22s. “Nothing has to change. You can stop.”
4. 22–28s. “Then answer, or don’t. The pause was the whole exercise.”

**Visual idea.** Close frame of a hand and a cup or a table edge. No light traveling up the arm, no chakra points, no waveform. A second shot of the person looking out a window is the offered alternative anchor, so the body is not mandatory. The phone stays in frame so the “before you react” situation is visible, without showing a fake Shanivar interface.

**Why it fits the stance.** Founder intention: embodied observation and conscious choice, not control of the mind. ST-004, still PROPOSED, says an external option matters and that stop is valid rather than resistance. The sadhak charter tells beginner-facing proposals to allow ordinary breathing, eyes-open alternatives, stopping, and no-change. This concept follows that instruction and refuses the contradicted breathwork story in founder item 3.

## 3. A pleasantness that is allowed to end

**Lived opening.** A color, a quiet second, or the end of a small task is mildly pleasant. Then the kettle, the street, or the next message is back. The pleasantness does not stay.

**Exact proposition.** A person may notice a brief pleasantness and let it end, without turning it into a duty to be happy or a sign of attainment. The sadhak inquiry README states that none of the sources reviewed there establishes permanent bliss, freedom from ordinary needs, or mind literally becoming colored energy. A useful proposed outcome in that file is more room to notice and choose. Pleasure is neither mandatory nor proof. This concept is that limit, stated as a public beat. It is not a report that contemplative joy occurred.

**Excluded claims.** Not liberation, awakening, equanimity as an achieved state, or “bliss without sleep, food, or other people.” Not the four joys of inner fire, not Mookerjee’s supreme bliss, not oceanic boundlessness from the breathwork study. Not “you should feel joy if you do this correctly.” Not a mood cure. Tradition-specific accounts of joy remain unread at passage level (LM-003 is still the open question). Do not invent a quotation from those traditions.

**Format.** One 20–30 second vertical concept, or the last card of a carousel that would otherwise drift into a bliss promise. Prefer this as a constraint card inside concept 1 or 2, not as a third growth post, unless the manager has a reason to run it alone.

**20–30 second outline.**

1. 0–7s. A small pleasant moment: steam, a color, a finished cup. It is ordinary.
2. 7–15s. “You can notice that this is pleasant.”
3. 15–23s. “You do not have to keep it, and you do not have to feel it.”
4. 23–28s. “Let the next ordinary thing return. Stopping here is not failure.”

**Visual idea.** The pleasant frame is allowed to cut, hard, to a duller ordinary frame: dishes, a doorway, a bus stop. No halo that lingers, no gold light, no white oval around the body (that image is in the founder summary of Clement and is not used). No before/after face of ecstasy.

**Why it fits the stance.** ST-005 and ST-008, both PROPOSED, warn against attainment scores and against treating short observation as lasting transformation. The charter separates temporary pleasant absorption from everyday functioning and from tradition-specific liberation. Putting “it is allowed to end” in the concept is the check against the permanent-bliss line the founder summary drifted toward and the ledger rejected.

## What these are not

They are not behavior-change evidence. Not found: a test that any of these glances changes a later reply, a return visit, or time spent in an app. They are not a commercial offer: they do not show Routine, Sadhana, alarms, or focus, and they have no destination URL because none was verified for this brief. An interesting concept, a behaviorally effective product, and a commercially viable product are three different claims. Only the first is even being proposed, and only for review.
