# SHN-COLOR-01 — A shade can mean nothing

Status: **Internal draft for adversarial review**. Owner: Social Media Manager. Research input: accepted execution of GROK-001 as a proposed handoff. This is not approved, rendered, scheduled or published.

## Purpose

Test whether Shanivar can make an ordinary moment of noticing feel clear and non-diagnostic. This is an editorial comprehension test, not a test of meditation efficacy, behavior change or product demand.

Audience moment: an adult has been looking at a bright phone and notices an ordinary colored object already in the room. Objective: communicate that attention can move without requiring a special result. Learning question: do viewers understand that “nothing comes to mind” is a valid ending?

## Exact 24-second Reel draft

| Time | Picture | Spoken and burned-in text |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0–4s | Phone fills the frame. The person lowers it slightly; daylight and a plain ceramic cup enter view. | “The brightest color in the room was your phone.” |
| 4–9s | Static close shot of the cup beside a sleeve and neutral wall. No object is highlighted digitally. | “Look up. Which shade holds your attention for a moment?” |
| 9–15s | Two ordinary objects remain visible. The person does not perform a reaction. | “Maybe it reminds you of something.” |
| 15–20s | Cut to the same quiet frame with no person and no visual emphasis. | “Maybe it means nothing.” |
| 20–24s | Wider view of the room; the phone stays face down. | “That is a complete answer. You can leave it there.” |

No additional voiceover. Do not add a breathing cue, instruction to close the eyes, promise of calm, or product-availability line.

## Caption draft

Sometimes a color brings a place, object or memory to mind. Sometimes it does not.

If you want, notice one ordinary shade around you for a moment. There is nothing to decode, diagnose or achieve. “Nothing came to mind” is a complete answer.

Then return to your day.

Illustrative scene created for Shanivar. It does not depict a customer experience.

## Evidence and exclusions

Background source: Palmer and Schloss’s ecological valence theory associates color preference with affective responses to color-associated objects in that study. It did not test this Reel, a momentary glance, meditation, attention transfer, behavior change or Shanivar. Do not cite the study on screen as proof of the invitation.

Excluded: color healing, alpha-wave control, aura/chakra diagnosis, spiritual rank, phototherapy analogy, treatment, permanent bliss, high-ventilation breathwork, subtle-body instruction, “hidden secret” framing, user outcome or commercial validation.
