## Why

The brief already does the things GROK-001 and the Codex acceptance required. It keeps a 20–24 second nothing ending, forbids a breathing cue, a promise of calm, a product-availability line, an on-screen study citation, and rainbow, aura, chakra, halo, traveling-light, medical, waveform, brain-scan, and before/after imagery. It says the piece is an editorial comprehension test, not a test of meditation efficacy, behavior change, or product demand. Those fences match `SHANIVAR_STANCE.md` ST-004 and ST-008 as still-PROPOSED cautions, the marketing operating system's rule that proposed ST entries are not doctrine, and the Codex review's condition that Palmer and Schloss stay off screen.

The draft still fails its own stance in the public words and the last picture. The burned-in hook, "The brightest color in the room was your phone," tells the viewer who they are. `grok-concepts.md` had kept that sentence as a scene ("The brightest color in the room is the phone"). The playbook says to reveal a contradiction the reader can recognise without being told who they are, not to manufacture anxiety about scrolling, and to end before explaining the reader to themselves. "Your phone" is that explanation.

The action line makes the shade the agent: "Which shade holds your attention for a moment?" The accepted concept asked which shade the person lingers on, and the caption later says "If you want." The Reel's own burned-in text is imperative ("Look up") and does not carry the no-change permission. The words that do carry it are intact and should stay: "Maybe it means nothing." and "That is a complete answer. You can leave it there."

The 20–24 second picture undoes those words. "Wider view of the room; the phone stays face down" will be read as the completed action of putting the phone down. "You can leave it there" then means leave the phone, not leave a nothing-answer. That is generic digital-detox mindfulness and an implied behavior change. GROK-001 already separated an interesting concept from behavior change and from a viable product. The brief's purpose states the same separation, then the last frame depicts a changed phone posture.

The caption is otherwise careful. "There is nothing to decode, diagnose or achieve" is a negation, not a diagnosis. "Illustrative scene created for Shanivar. It does not depict a customer experience" denies a testimonial. It does not say the app is available. The operating system still says Steady is in development for the current batch, and decision 0014 is not what this Reel shows. The hashtag `#NoticeAndChoose` is the caption's drift: it pairs noticing with choosing, while the spoken ending says nothing and leaving are complete. Founder intention records conscious choice as an intention, not an established outcome.

The evidence sentence is a little stronger than the local record. The sadhak inquiry README and the SI-10 note in `grok-independent-review.md` say Palmer and Schloss tested an account in which associations with objects help explain preferences in that study. Access recorded there was the abstract and the article's discussion of the theory, not a new full-text read. Codex called it background about associations and preference. This brief says the theory "associates color preference with affective responses to color-associated objects." "Affective responses" is not the ledger sentence. The title phrase "ecological valence theory" is in the recorded paper title, so naming it is not an invention, but the gloss should not get sharper than the files that were actually used. This pass did not reopen the PNAS paper.

## Phrases that could imply the forbidden readings

Live implications in the public draft:

- "The brightest color in the room was your phone." Shame and a surveillance-like reading of the viewer's attention. Not a medical or chakra diagnosis.
- "Look up." A command. Mild. The permission to do nothing is not in this line.
- "Which shade holds your attention for a moment?" The shade acts on attention. Not a subtle-body mechanism. It is the wrong subject for a piece that is supposed to leave authority with the person.
- "the phone stays face down" together with "You can leave it there." The pair can mean a phone-down outcome. The spoken sentence alone does not promise calm, bliss, or a measured change.
- "Test whether Shanivar can make an ordinary moment of noticing feel clear and non-diagnostic." "Feel clear" is a soft experiential aim. The same paragraph fences it as editorial comprehension, not efficacy. Keep the fence; do not let "feel clear" survive into a caption.
- `#NoticeAndChoose`. Implies that noticing produces a choice.

Checked and not counted as positive claims, because they deny the harm:

- "Maybe it reminds you of something." Hedged association. Not a reading of the person.
- "Maybe it means nothing."
- "That is a complete answer. You can leave it there." Pass as words. Fail only when pictured as a face-down phone.
- "There is nothing to decode, diagnose or achieve."
- "Nothing came to mind" is a complete answer.
- "It does not depict a customer experience."
- "No fake Shanivar interface or app-use claim."
- "Do not cite the study on screen as proof of the invitation."
- The exclusion list: color healing, alpha-wave control, aura/chakra diagnosis, spiritual rank, phototherapy analogy, treatment, permanent bliss, high-ventilation breathwork, subtle-body instruction, hidden-secret framing, user outcome, commercial validation.

No sentence in the draft offers a product for sale, a medical effect, an aura, a chakra, or a secret. The problem is the phone address and the phone-down picture, not a spiritual mechanism.

## One revised script

This is the only revision. Do not treat it as one of several variants. The 20–24 second nothing ending is unchanged on purpose.

| Time | Picture | Spoken and burned-in text |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0–4s | Phone fills the frame. It lowers slightly; daylight and a plain ceramic cup enter view. | "The brightest color in this frame was the phone." |
| 4–9s | Static close shot of the cup beside a sleeve and neutral wall. No object is highlighted digitally. | "If you want, which ordinary shade do you linger on?" |
| 9–15s | Two ordinary objects remain visible. The person does not perform a reaction. | "Maybe it reminds you of something." |
| 15–20s | Cut to the same quiet frame with no person and no visual emphasis. | "Maybe it means nothing." |
| 20–24s | Wider view of the same room. The phone may remain visible. It is not turned face down, and no posture is presented as the result. | "That is a complete answer. You can leave it there." |

No additional voiceover. Do not add a breathing cue, an instruction to close the eyes, a promise of calm, a feeling requirement, or a product-availability line.

Caption that travels with this script:

Sometimes a color brings a place, object or memory to mind. Sometimes it does not.

If you want, notice one ordinary shade around you for a moment. There is nothing to decode, diagnose or achieve. "Nothing came to mind" is a complete answer.

Then return to your day.

Illustrative scene created for Shanivar. It does not depict a customer experience.

#Shanivar #EverydayAttention

Evidence sentence to use instead of the current one, still off screen:

Background source, as recorded for SI-10: Palmer and Schloss tested an account in which associations with objects help explain color preferences in that study. It did not test this Reel, a momentary glance, meditation, attention transfer, behavior change, or Shanivar. Do not cite the study on screen as proof of the invitation.

## Distinctness

Not distinct enough to justify production in the current cut. Distinct enough to justify one text revision, not a new concept.

Brand fit of the nothing-ending is real. It matches the recorded founder intention to leave authority with the person, the README's warning that a color question must not become a test the viewer can fail, and the still-PROPOSED cautions in ST-002, ST-003, ST-004, and ST-008. It does not show Routine or Sadhana. Decision 0014 is a product-form decision, not a claim this Reel may advertise. Brand fit is not evidence, and it is not a release.

Evidence strength is background only. SI-10 does not test this glance, attention transfer, or Shanivar. The local files read here contain no user test, no metric, and no performance for this ID.

Likely performance is unknown. The playbook's 15–25 second band is a Shanivar experiment, not an Instagram rule, and 24 seconds sits inside that band. The playbook also says not to declare a winner from one post, and the operating system says views and saves are not practice outcomes. A phone-down opener is crowded mindfulness. The only non-generic beat is that nothing is a complete answer. That beat is not visible if the last frame is a face-down phone.

The learning question, whether viewers understand that "nothing comes to mind" is a valid ending, must not be scored from watch time, saves, shares, or comments. Those signals do not answer it. No comprehension method is specified. Do not invent one in production.
