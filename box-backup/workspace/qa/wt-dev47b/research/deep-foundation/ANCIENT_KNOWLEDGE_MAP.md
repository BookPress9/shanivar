# Ancient knowledge map — Shanivar foundation V1

Status: **PROPOSED**, 29 September 2026. All product conclusions await founder review. This is a researched orientation with selected primary passages, not an exhaustive history or a claim to have read every named book. Evidence/access depth is recorded in [SOURCE_LEDGER_ANCIENT.md](SOURCE_LEDGER_ANCIENT.md).

## What the foundation can honestly support

Shanivar can invite people to notice a sensation, recognize a mental elaboration and choose a next action without deciding which account of consciousness they must accept. This is an original product stance. The traditions below supply distinct questions and disciplines; they do not jointly prove a single ancient psychology.

“Manovigyana” should be an editorial umbrella for inquiry into mind, never the name of one uniformly transmitted ancient science. Store tradition-qualified concepts underneath it. A Sanskrit or Pāli term is not made equivalent to a contemporary construct by translating both as “mind.”

## 1. Sāṃkhya: analysis is not the enemy of experience

The *Sāṃkhyakārikā* distinguishes cognition's instruments from *puruṣa*. Its *buddhi*, *ahaṃkāra* and *manas* have different roles; its multiple conscious subjects are not a single universal self. The body and mind fall within *prakṛti*. The guṇas are features of its ontological explanation, not three empirically validated personality classes. [A01; primary Sanskrit](https://gretil.sub.uni-goettingen.de/gretil/corpustei/transformations/html/sa_IzvarakRSNa-sAMkhyakArikA.htm).

**Original synthesis:** “Out of the head” must not mean anti-intellectualism. Sometimes a useful distinction makes experience clearer; sometimes more explanation prolongs avoidance. The app should offer one observation before an optional explanation, and should let a user discover that deliberate thinking is the appropriate next action.

Product use: distinguish “What happened?”, “What did you add?” and “What will you do?” without presenting those three prompts as a translation of three Sanskrit faculties. A screenshot of a guṇa score would be a false precision failure, not evidence of deep personalization.

## 2. Classical Yoga: discipline has an ethical and liberative setting

Pātañjala Yoga includes practice, dispassion, ethical restraints/observances and attentional training; it cannot be reduced to body postures or productivity. Its liberation frame differs from nondual recognition. A substantial scholarly account dates the compiled text around 325–425 CE and treats sūtra/bhāṣya together; traditional separate-author accounts persist. [A02; Bryant](https://iep.utm.edu/yoga/), [Birch on Maas's research](https://www.theluminescent.org/2017/06/patanjali-and-vyasa.html).

**Original synthesis:** Shani-inspired discipline can become returning consistently with care. It should not become defeating the mind. Design a return from distraction as a normal event, avoid “failed meditation,” and never imply that thought is moral impurity. An alarm action is a behavior scaffold, not a yogic initiation. App blocking cannot stand in for ethical discernment.

A useful progression moves from choosing a manageable anchor, to noticing wandering, to choosing how to resume an ordinary task. Do not claim that completing these steps accomplishes the sūtra's ultimate aim.

## 3. Upaniṣads and Vedāntas: shared textual references, divergent interpretations

The Upaniṣads are a historically layered corpus. Their dialogues, analogies and accounts of self are not one standardized curriculum. *Kaṭha* 1.3's chariot locates mind and understanding within a larger account of self and liberation. It is not a forecast of executive-function neuroscience. [A03; text](https://sacred-texts.com/hin/sbe15/sbe15012.htm), [Black's scholarly overview](https://iep.utm.edu/upanisad/).

Advaita, Viśiṣṭādvaita, Dvaita and Bhedābheda traditions disagree over identity, difference, world and divine dependence. Their use of overlapping source texts does not erase the dispute. [A04–06; Dalal](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/shankara/), [Ranganathan](https://iep.utm.edu/ramanuja/), [Stoker](https://iep.utm.edu/madhva/), [Nicholson](https://iep.utm.edu/bhedabheda-vedanta/).

**Original synthesis:** a short practice may remain metaphysically open while its optional source page remains explicit. “Notice that a thought appeared” can be investigable; “you have now discovered the eternal witness” is an imposed interpretation. Religious framing can be available by informed choice without becoming the default test of success. Do not strip devotion from its source to repackage it as a self-optimization technique.

## 4. Buddhist frameworks: plurality within the comparison

MN 10's body/feeling/mind/dhamma observations and MN 118's breath training occur within a wider path. MN 18 gives an account of perceptual elaboration; SN 22.59 interrogates identification with the aggregates. These cannot be casually merged with a permanent witness doctrine. [A07–08; MN 10](https://accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/mn/mn.010.than.html), [MN 18](https://accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/mn/mn.018.than.html), [SN 22.59](https://accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/sn/sn22/sn22.059.nymo.html).

Abhidharma traditions systematize experience differently; Yogācāra elaborates a model including store consciousness, with contested interpretations of its idealism. Madhyamaka's critique of intrinsic nature is not simply a competing eight-part psychology. [A09; Ronkin](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/abhidharma/), [Szanyi](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/yogacara/), [Hayes](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/madhyamaka/).

**Original synthesis:** use “pleasant / unpleasant / neither / unsure” as a small observation option, with an origin note when relevant. Do not equate this with an exhaustive emotional inventory. Do not tell a person that everyday agency is unreal or a distressing sense of disconnection is spiritual progress. Distinguishing a present sensory event from a story about it is a design hypothesis inspired by several frameworks, not proof that they share an ontology.

## 5. Śaiva and Śākta traditions: not one generic Tantra

Sanderson's selected historical material differentiates ascetic and mantra traditions, Siddhānta and goddess/Bhairava systems, and contextualizes ritual institutions through patronage and social history. Śrīvidyā/Tripurasundarī, Kubjikā, Krama and Trika must not become synonyms. [A10; Sanderson, especially pp. 45–49](https://zenodo.org/records/4395629).

“Kashmir Shaivism” is useful as an orientation label, but an app record should identify a particular text, commentary and interpretive lineage. VBT, a recognition argument, a hymn and an initiation manual are different genres. The Ratié chapter is a strong next research lead for Pratyabhijñā, but only its metadata/abstract was available here: detailed philosophical extraction remains pending [A11].

**Original synthesis:** embodiment does not mean every physical sensation is a spiritual message. A source may interpret embodied experience within divine manifestation; the app can invite an observation while explaining that theology separately. Never turn tingling, breath discomfort or insomnia into automatic claims about kuṇḍalinī. Never infer practice readiness from engagement, astrology or a streak.

## 6. VBT: one important branch, not the entire foundation

The [existing VBT catalogue](../shanivar-vbt-breathing-catalog-2026-09-25.md) remains the detailed inventory. Its eight Steady seeds are adaptations, not eight fully transmitted traditional methods. Fresh review reconfirms that contemporary source interpretations can prescribe retention/subtle-energy visualization, which the beginner implementation deliberately omits. That omission is a documented change of scope, not evidence that the source contains no such instructions. [A12–14; Wallis 24–27](https://hareesh.org/blog/2022/11/30/vijana-bhairava-tantra-verses-22-27-the-foundational-practice).

Preserve disputed readings, especially v.55, and closing passage qualifications. A “112 techniques” marketing claim must not imply 112 validated breathwork interventions. Do not mechanically assign each verse to an emotion or time of day. Traditional textual provenance and the modern recommendation hypothesis are separate records.

## 7. Transmission research without a predetermined decline narrative

Source survival, practice continuity, institutional power and public visibility are different historical questions. Sanderson's work uses inscriptions and prescriptive literature to argue for complex institutional adaptation; it does not justify a single account of all later decline. Strube's colonial-Bengal research abstract emphasizes interactions among Indian scholars, colonial discourse and global interlocutors. [A10](https://zenodo.org/records/4395629); [A15](https://ochs.org.uk/lecture/modern-sakta-identities-in-a-global-context-bengali-tantrikas-and-john-woodroffe/).

**Research method proposed:** choose a region, community, text family and period before explaining change. Compare manuscript colophons, inscriptions, patronage records, translation histories and living-lineage testimony. Record whether a claim concerns destruction, reduced patronage, movement, oral continuity, print transformation or modern reinterpretation. Missing manuscripts do not establish vanished practice; a surviving title does not establish unchanged practice. Allow multiple mechanisms to coexist. No founder-facing or social copy should tell a totalizing “lost wisdom finally recovered by our app” story.

## Open decisions

Founder: accept a plural, tradition-labelled foundation or explicitly choose a doctrinal affiliation? The research recommends plurality without pretending neutrality erases provenance. Decide whether devotional practices belong in a later opt-in branch; they should not be silently secularized into today's Steady library. Determine who signs off source fidelity independently of product usability. Decide how corrections reach existing users if an origin note changes.

Next scholarship must deepen primary-edition comparison, especially VBT, Pratyabhijñā and Vedānta; current access limitations are evidence boundaries, not reasons to invent details. No new practice, source quotation, app navigation or notification is released by this report.
