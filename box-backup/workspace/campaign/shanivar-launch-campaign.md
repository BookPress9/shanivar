## The idea in five lines
1. One of Saturn's oldest Sanskrit names is *Śanaiścara*, "the one who moves slowly". It takes about 29.4 years to go around the sun, yet one Saturn day is only 10.7 hours (NASA). Fast inside, slow on the path.
2. Shanivar means Saturday, Shani's day. The campaign treats Saturn as a lens, not a force: time, patience, consequence, boundaries, return.
3. The world moves and you notice. Every reel puts one moment of stillness inside motion, then asks the viewer to try it for real: watch the reach, wait 30 seconds, take 20 slow steps, do one minute of nothing.
4. There is no fear and no fortune-telling. Shani is never the punisher. At most he is the receipt for what you repeated.
5. The line: **Slow is not late.** Supporting lines: *Be the slow one.* *Some things can wait.*

# 1. One-page launch positioning

**For** India-based, English-comfortable adults aged about 18–34 who work, study or create on screens (secondary: 31–44 balancing work and care). They admire discipline, keep postponing their own life, and already know which apps take more than they give.

**Shanivar is** an Android app in development that turns Saturn's old lessons into small acts you can do today:
- one chosen boundary on the apps that can wait,
- one deliberate minute,
- one honest line about what pulled you away.

**Unlike** meditation apps that sell calm, astrology pages that sell prediction, and self-help that sells motivation, **Shanivar offers responsibility you can practise and come back to.**

**Campaign line:** *Slow is not late.*
**Signature:** *Be the slow one.*
**Product line (launch assets only):** *Some things can wait.* This is in-app copy in the current build.

# 2. Three creative territories and the recommendation

## Territory A: The Slow Planet ★ recommended
**Concept.** Saturn spins in 10.7 hours but takes about 29.4 years to circle the sun. Its old name, *Śanaiścara*, means "the one who moves slowly". Your days spin fast too. The territory stages one slow person inside a fast world and dares the viewer to be slow for 10 seconds, 20 steps or one minute.
**Core line.** *Slow is not late.* / *Be the slow one.*
**Visual identity.**
- Speed against stillness in the same frame: Jaipur traffic, a hand still on a table, a long take on feet on stone steps.
- Abrupt cuts for automatic behaviour; long takes for observation.
- Sound drops out from city noise to footsteps alone.
- Clocks, shadows sliding across walls, the chai-ring stain as a home-made Saturn ring, the thin gold ring mark on black.
- Palette: deep black `#0b0d0b`, bone `#f7f5ee`, pewter and graphite, brass/gold `#c9aa73` used sparingly.
- No galaxies, nebulae or stock planets.
**Tone.** Calm, deadpan, occasionally funny.
**Strengths.**
- Rooted in verifiable facts (etymology, NASA).
- Built for the active-passive motion signature.
- Phone-reproducible anywhere.
- Patience without fear.
**Risk.** Too slow and solemn. *Mitigation:* every third reel is comedic and every reel ends with a physical act.

## Territory B: Child of the Shadow
**Concept.** In the old story (internal source: Vishnu Purana III.2; never named in public copy), Saturn's mother is Chhāyā, "shadow". Your shadow self is the automatic one: the fridge-opener, the "five more minutes", the agree-in-the-meeting, complain-in-the-car self. The territory exposes the automatic self and asks you to catch it once.
**Core line.** *Meet the one who follows you.*
**Visual identity.**
- Hard single-source light, silhouettes, shadows on walls doing what the person does a beat earlier.
- Night interiors, close-ups of hands and eyes, near-black frames with one warm practical lamp.
**Tone.** Tension, intimacy, confession.
**Strengths.** Strong Expose; hook from a real traditional story (told without naming the text); great for uncomfortable admissions.
**Risk.** It can tip into horror and "dark for dark's sake", or read as Jungian pop-psychology. It also risks implying Shani is ominous. *Mitigation:* use it as a motif inside Territory A, not as the master idea.

## Territory C: The Receipt (Saturday Court)
**Concept.** Tradition links Shani to karma. *Karma* means action. Consequences are receipts for what you repeated. A deadpan Saturday "court" where your excuses testify: the 2 a.m. reel, the unopened course, the 47 snoozes.
**Core line.** *Not a curse. A receipt.*
**Visual identity.**
- Thermal-printer receipts, timestamps, flat-lay tables.
- Bright, absurd, symmetrical frames with comic timing.
- Brass bell, wooden gavel replaced by a steel tumbler.
**Tone.** Humour, everyday absurdity.
**Strengths.** Very shareable, comment-friendly, and kills the fear myth.
**Risks.**
- The "judge" framing slides towards punishment and simplistic karmic reward.
- It is tonally furthest from the established dark editorial voice.
- It needs props and staging.
*Mitigation:* use it once (R06) as a comedic register, not as the campaign.

## Recommendation: Territory A, The Slow Planet, with B and C as motifs
1. **Truth at the core.** The hook rests on a dictionary etymology, a Puranic genealogy and NASA numbers. Nothing in it is a supernatural claim.
2. **It is the motion signature.** "The world moves, the viewer notices" is the territory itself, not a style layered on top.
3. **No fear, still weight.** "Slow" carries discipline, patience and consequence without punishment.
4. **Natural product bridge.** Protected time ("Some things can wait."), "One deliberate minute" and "Continuity, not a streak." are in-app lines that say *slow is not late* in product form.
5. **Un-copyable.** A generic meditation app can't post "Be the slow one" without the Shani/Shanivar link that gives it meaning.
6. **Fits the existing voice:** "The world is loud. Be here." / "Let the world wait" / "You woke up. Then the world walked in."
7. **Phone-first.** Every shot can be made on one Android phone in Jaipur.

## Five-line summary
- **Territory:** The Slow Planet. Saturn spins fast but travels slow, and its oldest name means "the one who moves slowly".
- **Line:** *Slow is not late.* Signature: *Be the slow one.*
- **Look:** speed against stillness, abrupt cuts against long takes, sound drop-outs, shadows and clocks, black/bone/brass, no cosmic stock.
- **Mechanic:** every reel exposes one specific automatic behaviour, reframes it through a sourced Saturn idea, then makes the viewer do one slow act now.
- **Why:** truthful, fear-free, phone-reproducible, and the only frame where the product (protected time, one deliberate minute, no streaks) is the same idea as the brand.
# 3. Research: four-layer concept map and sources

**INTERNAL: not for public copy.** Text names, verses and translators on this page are for accuracy checks only. Under the Naming rule (positioning page), public copy may name Shani/Saturn but never a classical text or teacher.

Every concept separates four layers:
- **(1) Source:** what a text or study actually says.
- **(2) Tradition:** what devotional or astrological tradition associates with Shani.
- **(3) Psychology:** what the evidence supports.
- **(4) Shanivar:** our creative interpretation.

Quotes are copied from the fetched translation. Public-domain translations are preferred so that no living teacher is named in public copy. Astrology is never presented as science.

## C1. Slowness and time: "the one who moves slowly"
- **Source.** Monier-Williams (via Wisdomlib): *Śani*, "[probably] 'slow-moving'; cf. *manda*", the planet Saturn, and also "Saturday". *Śanaiścara* is the classical name. NASA: Saturn completes an orbit in "about 29.4 Earth years", and "one day on Saturn takes only 10.7 hours".
- **Tradition.** Saturn is the slowest of the five planets watched by the ancients. Saturday (Shanivar) is Shani's day. Tibetan Buddhist iconography (Niṣpannayogāvalī, cited on Wisdomlib) shows Śanaiścara riding a tortoise, "the slowest animal for the slowest planet".
- **Psychology.** Habit strength grows asymptotically. It plateaued after an average of 66 days in the study summarised by Gardner, Lally & Wardle (2012). Change is slow and uneven.
- **Shanivar.** *Slow is not late.* Being "the slow one" means seeing the reach before it happens.

## C2. Shadow and automaticity: Chhāyā's son
- **Source.** Vishnu Purana III.2 (Wilson, 1840): Sanjñā, unable to bear the sun's heat, leaves Chhāyā ("shadow") in her place. "The sun, supposing Chháyá to be his wife Sanjná, begot by her three other children, Śanaiścara (Saturn), another Manu (Sávarńi), and a daughter Tapatí."
- **Tradition.** One of Shani's epithets is *Chhāyāsuta*, "son of Chhāyā" (listed on Wikipedia, tertiary).
- **Psychology.** Trait self-control relates more strongly to *automatic* than to controlled behaviour (de Ridder et al. 2012, meta-analysis of 102 studies, N = 32,648). Habits are "externally-triggered automatic responses to frequently encountered contexts" (Gardner et al. 2012).
- **Shanivar.** Your shadow habits aren't the enemy. They are the part of you that hasn't been watched yet. *Symbolic reading, not doctrine.*

## C3. Karma as action, not reward
- **Source.** Bhagavad Gita 2.47 (K. T. Telang, SBE 8, 1882): "Your business is with action alone; not by any means with fruit. Let not the fruit of action be your motive (to action). Let not your attachment be (fixed) on inaction." Gita 3.8 (Telang): "action is better than inaction."
- **Tradition.** Shani is widely described as associated with karma, time and justice, giving results according to thought, speech and deed. Popular culture adds fear (Sade Sati, the "gaze" curse story). *We do not use the fear.*
- **Psychology.** A medium-to-large change in intention (d = 0.66) produced only a small-to-medium change in behaviour (d = 0.36) across 47 experiments (Webb & Sheeran 2006). Implementation intentions work by making the cue and response accessible, not by boosting motivation (Webb & Sheeran 2008).
- **Shanivar.** *Not a curse. A receipt for what you repeated.* The only part you hold is the next action.

## C4. Bearing discomfort and the urge
- **Source.** Gita 2.14 (Telang): "The contacts of the senses... which produce cold and heat, pleasure and pain, are not permanent, they are ever coming and going. Bear them."
- **Tradition.** Shani is associated with endurance, hardship borne and maturity.
- **Psychology.** Distress tolerance is studied as the capacity to withstand aversive states. The review notes "conceptual and operational heterogeneity" (Leyro, Zvolensky & Bernstein 2010). In one RCT (N = 123 student smokers), urge-surfing instructions did **not** reduce urges, but participants smoked fewer cigarettes over 7 days (Bowen & Marlatt 2009). That is a single study, so it is preliminary.
- **Shanivar.** *The urge is weather. Watch it pass.* This is a practice invitation, not a treatment.

## C5. The good and the pleasant: scrolling
- **Source.** Katha Upanishad I.2.1–2 (F. Max Müller, SBE 15): "The good is one thing, the pleasant another... the wise prefers the good to the pleasant." Gita 18.37–38 (Telang): one happiness is "like poison first and comparable to nectar in the long run". Another, from the senses, is "at first comparable to nectar and in the long run like poison". Gita 2.62–63 (Telang): "The man who ponders over objects of sense forms an attachment to them; from (that) attachment is produced desire; and from desire anger is produced..."
- **Tradition.** Shani is associated with restraint and delayed reward.
- **Psychology.** Task aversiveness, task delay, self-efficacy and impulsiveness are the strongest predictors of procrastination (Steel 2007, 691 correlations). Procrastination is often "giving priority to short-term mood repair over achieving long-term goals" (Zhang, Liu & Feng 2019). Phone notifications alone disrupted an attention task, comparable to active phone use (Stothart et al. 2015).
- **Shanivar.** *Sweet first, bitter later. You know this taste.*
- **Do NOT claim:** that the mere presence of a phone "drains your brain". A pre-registered replication of Ward et al. 2017 found no effect (Ruiz Pardo & Minda 2022).

## C6. Silence: not tolerating stillness
- **Source.** Gita 6.35 (Telang): "the mind is difficult to restrain, and fickle. Still... it may be restrained by constant practice and by indifference (to worldly objects)."
- **Tradition.** Not specific to Shani. It is used only as supporting text.
- **Psychology.** Across 11 studies, people did not enjoy 6–15 minutes alone with their thoughts, and "many preferred to administer electric shocks to themselves". In one study, 12 of 18 men and 6 of 24 women shocked themselves at least once (Wilson et al. 2014).
- **Shanivar.** *Silence is loud the first time.* One deliberate minute is an initiation, not an achievement.

## C7. Practice over motivation, and returning after a miss
- **Source.** Yoga Sutra 1.13–1.14 (BonGiovanni translation, already in Sources): "Practice is the effort to secure steadiness. This practice becomes well-grounded when continued with reverent devotion and without interruption over a long period of time." Yoga Sutra 2.1: "Austerity, the study of sacred texts, and the dedication of action to God constitute the discipline..."
- **Tradition.** The Purana Index (via Wisdomlib, citing Matsya Purāṇa 11.9, 38) records that Śani "attained the status of a planet by penance". **Not verified in a primary translation; do not use publicly until checked.**
- **Psychology.**
  - "Missing the occasional opportunity to perform the behaviour did not seriously impair the habit formation process" (Gardner et al. 2012, summarising Lally et al. 2010).
  - The ego-depletion ("willpower runs out") effect was d = 0.04, 95% CI [−0.07, 0.15], across 23 labs (Hagger et al. 2016).
- **Shanivar.** The sutra asks for no break, and the study says one break doesn't undo you. We hold both: *long time, and come back.* "Continuity, not a streak." (in-app).

## C8. Postponing life and the future self
- **Source.** NASA: one Saturn orbit takes about 29.4 years.
- **Tradition.** The pop-astrology "Saturn return" (around ages 29–30) is mentioned only to step away from it. **It is not science, and we never use it as a prediction.**
- **Psychology.** When the future self feels similar, vivid and positive, people make more future-benefiting choices (Hershfield 2011, review).
- **Shanivar.** *In 29 years Saturn will be back here. Where will you be?* This is a reflective question, not a forecast.

## C9. Escaping discomfort and blaming
- **Source.** Gita 2.62–63 (the dwelling → desire → anger chain) applies to reacting.
- **Tradition.** Shani is associated with responsibility and consequences.
- **Psychology.** Experiential avoidance, meaning efforts to escape unwanted thoughts and feelings, is proposed as a dimension common to many problems (Hayes et al. 1996, conceptual review).
- **Shanivar.** The 30-second pause before replying is a **creative practice, not a tested intervention**. Say so if asked.

## Metric definitions
- **3-s hold** = viewers past 3 s ÷ plays. **Avg watch %** = avg watch time ÷ duration.
- **Share rate** = shares ÷ reach × 1,000. **Save rate** = saves ÷ reach × 1,000.
- **Meaningful comment** = a comment of ≥4 words that names a specific behaviour, an action taken, a number, or a reflection. Emojis, "nice", tags-only and spam don't count. **MC rate** = MC ÷ reach × 1,000.
- **Profile visit rate** = profile visits ÷ reach × 1,000. **Follow conversion** = follows ÷ profile visits.
- **Link CTR** = link taps ÷ profile visits. **Sign-up rate** = form submissions ÷ link taps (via UTM).
- **DM rate** = keyword DMs ÷ reach × 1,000.
- **Experiment completion** = participants reporting Day 7 (missed days allowed) ÷ SATURN DMs.
- **Activation** = testers with first boundary saved and first minute completed ÷ testers installed. **Repeat** = activated testers with practice on ≥3 of 7 days.
- **Views are recorded but never used alone as a success criterion.**

# 14. Testing plan: hooks, stories, practices

## Method
- **Preferred:** Instagram **Trial Reels** (shown to non-followers first), if available on the account. Post 2 variants of the same reel with only one variable changed. After 72 h, publish the winner to followers.
- **Fallback (matched pairs):** post variants on comparable weekdays and times (same slot ±1 h, same week), up to 2 pairs per week. Treat the results as directional; don't over-read a single pair.
- **One variable per test.** Every other element stays identical, including the cover, caption length and sound.
- **Decision rule:**
  - A variant "wins" if it beats the other by **≥20% on the test's primary metric** with reach of at least 1,000 each.
  - Otherwise: no decision. Retest or keep the cheaper version.
- **Weekly review:** Monday 10:00 IST (05:30 CET after 25 Oct). 30 minutes: fill metrics.csv, tag comments, write 3 learnings, choose next week's tests.

## CTA tests (P3–P4 only; primary metric: DM rate or link CTR)
- **C1:** "DM SATURN" vs "Comment SATURN" (then the founder DMs the rules manually).
- **C2:** "Link in bio" vs "DM WAIT" for waitlist sign-ups. Count both as a sign-up; record the drop-off.
- **C3:** Soft ("if you want it") vs direct ("Join the Android waitlist").

## Guardrails
- Never test truthfulness: no variant may exaggerate the product status or the results.
- Never test fear framing (e.g., "Shani is watching") even if it would "win".
- Stop any variant that draws comments offended on religious grounds. Review it with the founder and CB5.
