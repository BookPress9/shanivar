# Shanivar product–market fit

Current launch direction: [the two-entry MVP](../android-app/INITIAL_MVP_SCOPE_2026-09-13.md) serves family introductions with voluntary Shani-inspired framing and younger self-use for focus and awareness of thinking. It supersedes earlier broad launch recommendations in this report; the research remains background evidence, not a claim that the simplified build or its outcomes are verified.

**Strategic judgment:** Shanivar’s strongest candidate position is helping people recognize a recurring mental loop, make contact with their present experience, and choose what happens next. Its spiritual purpose gives that experience meaning. Its commercial case depends on whether people repeatedly find that experience useful enough to choose and pay for.

The opportunity is credible; product–market fit is unproven. Attention interruption, reflective conversation, astrology, meditation, and movement alarms all have established substitutes. Combining them creates an opportunity to connect experiences that are otherwise separate, but also creates complexity and conflicting incentives.

The most important question is: **When Shanivar interrupts a loop, does the person engage differently with life afterward?** Reading another personally compelling message is insufficient evidence.

## The spiritual purpose and the product promise

“Get out of a mental loop, attain presence, and live with cosmic flow” contains three different ambitions. Recognizing a loop can be described through a specific event. Presence is a first-person experience that can be reported imperfectly. Cosmic flow expresses a philosophical or spiritual orientation; this research does not establish an external measurement of cosmic alignment.

Keep all three, but give each a different role. The philosophy can articulate a less compulsive relationship with thought and a more receptive participation in life. The experience can invite a moment of noticing. The product claim can describe the actual question, practice, and choice Shanivar enables. None requires a promise to restore an earlier identity.

“Maya” should be treated with more care than a synonym for excessive phone use. Its meaning varies across philosophical contexts; scholarship on Śaṅkara describes ignorance, superimposition, and later developments in theories of māyā. Shanivar’s everyday language can be an explicitly contemporary interpretation, rather than claiming that Indian traditions reduce to one psychological mechanism. [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, Śaṅkara](https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/spr2026/entries/shankara/).

Likewise, do not use studies of psychological flow to certify “cosmic flow.” That would change the meaning of the evidence. A person can value the spiritual framing without the company claiming to measure enlightenment, destiny, or the cosmic correctness of a decision.

Suggested positioning for testing:

> **Notice the loop. Meet this moment. Choose your next step.**

Supporting description: “Shanivar offers a personal question and a small practice for moments when you feel caught in the same thought or reaction.” Use intended-experience wording until the corresponding public journey is verified.

This language leaves room for depth. The philosophical explanation can follow an experience of value, rather than becoming a prerequisite to understand the first screen. For spiritually receptive users, an optional explanation can connect recurring identification with the brand’s interpretation of maya. For other users, the same experience can remain grounded in their own language.

## What people might actually hire Shanivar to do

The audience is broad, but a useful entry problem is specific. A person usually encounters a product at a moment of friction, not at the level of an entire philosophy. The following are research hypotheses, not observed customer statements.

| Existing audience | Recurring situation to investigate | Possible job | Evidence needed |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Adult Gen Z | Reopening a feed after comparison or uncertainty about direction | Notice what is happening before consuming another answer | Recent examples; prior attempts to stop; repeated use of one intervention |
| Millennials | Carrying a work argument or unfinished task into home life | Put down one mental thread and enter the next interaction deliberately | A specific transition improved, rather than generic satisfaction |
| Parents/older adults | Replaying a worry or reacting from an established family role | Create room for a different response without being instructed by a child | Direct participant demand, comprehension, independent return |
| Younger adults connecting with parents | Repeated advice, interruption, or phone distraction during conversation | Share one moment of attention without trying to fix each other | Both parties welcome it; neither feels monitored or corrected |

**Recommended first learning cohort:** adults within those existing audiences who describe one recurring, everyday loop, have attempted a workaround, and are willing to test one ordinary transition. Select for the situation and existing effort. Record age/life stage so the team can discover differences instead of presuming them.

Do not pool incompatible jobs into one retention number. A student protecting study time, a parent seeking companionship, and a professional exploring astrology may all open Shanivar while valuing different products. A cross-generational mission can remain broad while the first experiment concentrates on one repeated problem.

The useful interview opener is “Walk me through the last time this happened.” Ask what the person did next, what the loop cost them, which alternatives they tried, and why those alternatives failed. Avoid introducing “maya,” “presence,” or “cosmic flow” before hearing their own description; otherwise the research will measure agreement with the brand’s vocabulary.

## The gaps Shanivar is attempting to address

### 1. Knowing without doing

A user can understand that scrolling or repetitive thinking is unhelpful while still acting automatically. Shanivar’s short practices and situational questions are plausible bridges from understanding to action. Published one sec research followed 280 participants over six weeks and reported a 57% reduction in actual target-app openings. This supports investigating interruption and deliberation; it does not establish Shanivar’s effectiveness or an equivalent reduction in screen time, rumination, or distress. [PNAS study record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36795756/).

The commercial weakness is that this mechanism is already available elsewhere. Shanivar must discover whether its particular question and subsequent action add value beyond a pause or block alone.

### 2. Interruption without a meaningful next step

Blocking prevents an action; it does not establish what the person will do instead. Shanivar can connect a chosen boundary to one personally relevant alternative: start the intended task, notice the room, or enter a conversation. The possible gap lies in the quality and repeatability of that transition.

This is narrower than claiming blockers offer no alternatives. Opal’s documented iOS block screens already include mindful offline actions, offline ideas, wisdom, and religious material. The proposed differentiation must therefore be tested against an existing substitute with meaningful content, not a deliberately weak “blank block screen” caricature. [Opal block-screen documentation](https://help.opalapp.com/article/how-to-choose-your-block-screens).

### 3. Feeling understood without changing a pattern

Chart interpretation and reflective AI can make an interaction feel personally relevant. Shanivar’s opportunity is to translate relevance into a small experiment in life and later ask whether the interpretation fit. The risk is an experience where users repeatedly feel described but do nothing differently.

The Pattern already markets personal insights, timing cycles, relationship interpretation, and in-depth conversation. These offerings disprove a simple claim that chart-informed self-understanding and conversation are untouched territory. They do not establish the psychological validity of chart interpretations. [The Pattern developer listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/the-pattern/id1071085727).

### 4. Practice separated from the moment it is needed

Shanivar’s 30–120-second practice tradition fits ordinary transitions better than requiring every user to establish a substantial meditation routine first. Nevertheless, contemplative awareness in daily life is also an existing category: Waking Up explicitly offers noticing, life transitions, meditation, and philosophical material. [Waking Up](https://www.wakingup.com/).

The test is whether Shanivar reaches a chosen moment with less setup and more personal usefulness. A large practice library is not necessary to establish this.

### 5. Discipline separated from presence

Focus boundaries and movement alarms can create conditions for a different start to the day. They do not prove attention or presence. Alarmy already offers step, squat, photo, and other dismissal missions, so movement is an established mechanism. [Alarmy](https://alar.my/en).

The potentially valuable Shanivar sequence is waking, entering the environment, and carrying out an intention selected beforehand. Count the alarm’s technical success separately from that subsequent behavior. An alarm dismissed by walking can coexist with immediately resuming unwanted scrolling.

## Market psychology: what makes a trigger useful

### Recognition must precede interpretation

A strong opening makes the user recognize a concrete moment without needing to accept a diagnosis: “You have reread the message. What are you hoping the next reading will settle?” This is an illustrative prompt for a user who has disclosed that situation, not a claim that Shanivar observes private messages.

A weak opening assigns an identity: “You are trapped in your ego.” It can sound profound while providing little actionable information. It can also position the product above the user as an authority on their inner state.

The proposed mechanism is recognition followed by a small departure from the pattern. Recognition alone may be commercially engaging; the departure is what connects engagement to the mission. Test both separately.

### Curiosity should resolve into experience

Loewenstein’s information-gap account describes curiosity in relation to a perceived gap in knowledge. Applied cautiously, a question can draw attention toward something the person has not noticed. It does not follow that withholding personal answers indefinitely is beneficial. [The Psychology of Curiosity, author-hosted paper](https://www.cmu.edu/dietrich/sds/docs/loewenstein/PsychofCuriosity.pdf).

For Shanivar, the useful unresolved question is “What happens if I pause before responding?” The answer becomes available through an experiment. “Unlock the next card to discover your hidden blockage” instead makes the app the gatekeeper of an explanation. That may intensify consumption while working against the desired relationship to thought.

### Challenge can be clear without becoming controlling

Self-determination theory identifies autonomy, competence, and relatedness as important conditions for motivation. Shanivar’s application is to preserve a real choice, make the action achievable, and offer a respectful sense of guidance. This is a design inference, not an assurance of retention. [Self-determination theory overview](https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/the-theory/).

Evidence on phrasing is nuanced. A 2007 experiment found more anger and poorer fairness judgments with controlling language; a 2019 experiment involving 521 participants found no significant effects of its message-frame manipulations on reactance or perceived autonomy support. The latter cautions against assuming that substituting “could” for “must” solves the design problem. [Miller et al.](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/j.1468-2958.2007.00297.x), [Altendorf et al.](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/2055207619832767).

A 2026 chatbot paper reports a tradeoff: polite feedback produced lower reactance and higher behavioral intentions but was perceived as less surprising and engaging; other styles created different mixtures. These are study-specific perceptions and intentions, not demonstrated long-term behavior change. The implication is to test a direct, respectful voice against a gentle one using the same practice and situation. [Cox, Wester, and van Berkel](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.20683).

An illustrative direct question: “Another explanation may keep you here. Would you try one moment without solving it?” An illustrative gentle question: “Could this wait while you notice one sound around you?” Either can fail if poorly timed or repeated too often.

### Choice needs a practical bridge

Implementation-intention research examines specifying when and how an intended action will happen. Shanivar can use a simple user-chosen plan: “When I finish work, I will put the phone down and notice the room before speaking.” This is more testable than a broad intention to live in flow. [Gollwitzer and Sheeran review](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0065-2601%2806%2938002-1).

The practice should remain small enough to perform immediately. A long explanation of presence may consume the very opportunity to experience it. A suggestion to notice a nearby sound or the support of a chair can also offer an alternative to breath-focused practice when that is not welcome.

### Relief, novelty, and agency are different rewards

Ask separately whether a message felt interesting, whether it offered temporary relief, and whether the person made a useful choice. A sharp horoscope may win the first. A reassuring chat may win the second. Neither necessarily wins the third.

Do not make calmness mandatory evidence of success. A person might become more aware of discomfort and still respond more deliberately. Conversely, feeling soothed while avoiding a necessary conversation may not represent the intended benefit. This is a proposed interpretation framework to validate with participants.

## Product tensions that could prevent fit

**The scroller can become another loop.** Requiring an answer before the next message introduces participation but does not establish awareness. Users can learn to tap through answers or seek repeated interpretations. Current source includes explicit ending and continuation controls; the unresolved question is how people use them. Recommend one clearly bounded practice episode with a useful ending, then evaluate whether further reflection helps or substitutes for action.

**Astrology can create relevance and identity rigidity at the same time.** Keep chart calculations, interpretation, and lived evidence distinguishable. Let people reject a theme. An interpretation that does not fit should be corrected or dropped, rather than reframed as proof of resistance. Make the chart an optional mode of inquiry so users can test the experience without accepting that explanatory system.

**A teacher can accidentally become an oracle.** The strongest teacher behavior invites the user to observe for themselves. Repeatedly supplying answers about who the user is can produce the opposite relationship. A good longitudinal record contains the user’s own revisions, including occasions when Shanivar was wrong.

**Control can conflict with flow.** A voluntarily selected lock can support an intention. Friction imposed without a clear prior choice can turn the experience into a contest against the product. Keep the terms understandable before starting. Do not require a private disclosure or an approved emotional response to end reflection or access ordinary release controls.

**Progression can become spiritual performance.** The older README and ontology describe consciousness stages, XP, and levels. Those are product metaphors, not validated measurements of spiritual development. Displaying rank as if it establishes greater awareness may introduce comparison into a product intended to loosen it. Prioritize descriptions of practice and user-reported changes over claims of advancement in consciousness.

**Technical friction can masquerade as rejection of the philosophy.** Permission failure, cold starts, inconsistent language, or an alarm not appearing can all cause abandonment. Instrument these separately before concluding that users rejected presence or astrology. Source implementation and automated tests still do not establish physical-device reliability across manufacturers.

## Under-served opportunities to investigate

These are ranked strategic hypotheses. “Under-served” means worth testing against current alternatives, not proven absence of supply.

| Priority | Opportunity | Small experiment | Disconfirming result |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | A useful exit from a self-identified loop | User selects one recurring situation; one question leads to one offline action | Messages feel insightful but reported actions do not repeat |
| 2 | Continuity based on user-confirmed experience | Next review recalls the user’s chosen experiment and asks whether it helped | Recall feels inaccurate, intrusive, or unnecessary |
| 3 | Re-entry after a lapse without rebuilding a routine | Offer a fresh small action after non-use, with no catch-up burden | Users ignore it because the underlying job is absent |
| 4 | Presence during role transitions | One end-of-work or family-conversation practice | A generic reminder works equally well with less friction |
| 5 | Guided inquiry that can fade | Let users request fewer prompts while retaining a periodic review | Prompt reduction reveals no continuing value and no desire for support |
| 6 | Shared intergenerational practice | Both people independently opt into one brief listening exercise | One participant experiences it as correction or family pressure |
| 7 | Reflection before delegating a meaningful choice to AI | A self-initiated “What is my judgment?” prompt before external advice | Too rare, too abstract, or already satisfied by existing habits |

The seventh opportunity fits a changing world but is more speculative. It should begin with interviews and a manual prototype, not a broad new AI-agent integration. “The world is changing quickly” provides a strategic question; it does not supply a market-size estimate or willingness to pay.

For intergenerational use, the parent must remain a customer with their own preferences. Avoid a model where the younger purchaser receives private reflection summaries or compliance reports. The valuable shared outcome would be the interaction itself, with both participants free to opt out.

## Why now, with limits

Microsoft’s 2025 Work Trend Index reports that 48% of surveyed employees describe work as chaotic and fragmented. This supports investigating work transitions, but its global knowledge-worker context does not establish demand across Shanivar’s Indian or older-adult audiences. [Microsoft report](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/worklab/work-trend-index/breaking-down-infinite-workday).

A 2025 randomized trial of blocking mobile internet reported improvements in attention and well-being outcomes. Adoption difficulty is as relevant as the benefits: of 467 participants who committed, 266 set up the app and 119 met the preregistered compliance criterion. That intervention was broader than Shanivar’s proposed targeted boundaries. It supports investigating lower-burden approaches, while leaving their comparative benefit unproven. [Castelo et al., PNAS Nexus](https://doi.org/10.1093/pnasnexus/pgaf017).

The business inference is that willingness to experience a constraint cannot be assumed from agreement that constant connection is a problem. Shanivar’s first-session friction may be a major determinant of whether the philosophy gets a chance to matter.

India should also not be treated as a homogeneous English-language market. The historical IAMAI/Kantar 2024 report documents substantial Indic-language preference. That supports listening for language fit, not launching broad multilingual generation before comprehension and quality are verified. No current Indian revenue market is sized from those internet-use figures. [IAMAI/Kantar report](https://www.iamai.in/sites/default/files/research/Kantar_%20IAMAI%20report_2024_.pdf).

## Validation and release sequence

### First: verify the problem and comprehension

Conduct 12 exploratory interviews distributed across the existing adult audience: four adult Gen Z, four Millennials, and four parents/older adults. These numbers are a practical learning allocation, not a representative sample. Interview intergenerational participants separately before inviting any shared exercise.

Capture the last episode, trigger, existing workaround, cost, and desired next action. Present a single prototype only after that account. Observe whether they understand the question, attempt the action, and can explain what Shanivar is for without repeating brand language. A useful negative finding is that the person enjoys the philosophy but has no recurring job for the product.

### Second: establish a dependable small experience

Use six participants for technical and comprehension shakedown, then a separate 14-person learning cohort if those issues are resolved. This follows the existing small-cohort direction without treating it as a powered efficacy study. Match the chosen recurring situation, record life stage, and analyze technical failures separately.

The first experience should require one chosen situation, one question, one small action, and one optional check-in. Test birth details and advanced permission requests only where necessary to the selected experience. Keep a clear distinction between a source-available capability and a capability verified on that person’s device.

For a loop-interruption pilot, use a short baseline followed by two weeks of the same experience and a later follow-up. Baseline and follow-up reports help interpret change but do not create causal proof. A deliberate reduction in prompts can explore whether value carries over; it cannot establish permanent habit formation.

### Third: test one incremental ingredient

Compare a simple situational cue with the same cue plus one personally relevant reflective question. Keep timing, action, and other features consistent. With a small cohort, inspect individual episodes and qualitative evidence; report uncertainty rather than announcing a statistically established winner. If a larger causal comparison becomes justified, plan sample size from a defined effect and observed variability.

Do not simultaneously vary the chart, tone, alarm, block intensity, and practice length. If the combined bundle works, that design would still leave the reason unknown. Later isolate chart-based framing from personalization based only on user-provided context, allowing explicit preference and correction.

### Fourth: test payment after useful repetition

Ask for an actual paid continuation only after a user has experienced recurring value. Offer a clearly described service at a stated price, then record acceptance, actual payment, continued use, cancellation, and reasons. A willingness-to-pay interview is weaker evidence than a transaction, and a transaction without continuing value is weaker than retention.

A bounded paid practice program and an ongoing support subscription are both plausible hypotheses. The former aligns with a specific learning period; the latter must justify recurring delivery and review. Do not sell permanent liberation and then rely on indefinite dependence. Users can also value continued scaffolding without that proving lasting internal change.

There is no evidence-based price recommendation yet. Build a simple contribution calculation from actual payment, applicable transaction costs, model/delivery cost, and support time. Compare acquisition expense with observed retention before interpreting top-line signups as commercial fit.

## Measurement that matches the mission

The proposed primary learning measure is **the number of enrolled participants reporting useful loop interruptions on at least three separate days in a week**. “Useful” means they can identify a particular episode and a chosen action afterward. Three days is a provisional team decision rule, not a validated scientific or industry threshold.

Report that number alongside all enrolled participants, all technically activated participants, and missing check-ins. Missing reports are unknown, not automatically failure or success. Self-report remains self-report even when an app event confirms a prompt was shown.

| Layer | Example measure | Interpretation limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Technical exposure | Prompt displayed; lock actually active; alarm fired | Delivery is not attention |
| Participation | Practice started; response supplied | A tap is not proof of practice |
| Reported episode | User identifies noticing and a subsequent action | Recall and demand effects remain possible |
| Repeat value | Useful episodes across separate days; voluntary return | Can depend on prompts or novelty |
| Commercial value | Paid continuation and renewal | Payment alone does not validate spiritual outcomes |
| Counter-signals | Feeling judged; inaccurate interpretation; unwanted prolonged use | Must be examined even when engagement rises |

Track time in Shanivar diagnostically, not as the goal. More time may mean useful reflection or a new loop. Less time may mean successful transfer into life or abandonment. Interpret both through episode-level evidence and voluntary follow-up.

Do not infer an offline action solely because the app moved to the background. With consent and feasible instrumentation, device behavior may corroborate a narrow claim about app use; it cannot establish that a person was present, compassionate, or in cosmic flow.

## Release decisions and positioning tests

The next release should make the proposed core sequence concrete before expanding the number of spiritual explanations or screens. Prioritize a dependable encounter, a bounded ending, an explicit way to disagree, and a review of what the person actually experienced. Retain existing broader capabilities while deciding which ones the pilot must exercise; this report does not authorize deleting them.

Three draft messages can test different doors into the same experience. They are original prototypes, not customer quotes or efficacy claims:

| Door | Draft | What to learn |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Everyday recognition | “The same thought again. Before following it, notice one sound in the room.” | Does the person recognize a useful moment and try the action? |
| Direct inquiry | “Will another answer change this moment—or can you try one different step?” | Does challenge help action without increasing rejection? |
| Spiritual invitation | “For a moment, let the story rest. What is here before you explain it?” | Does spiritual resonance support practice beyond initial interest? |

Test comprehension and action, then repetition. Likes, saves, provocative replies, and a feeling of being understood are useful acquisition signals, but they cannot decide which version fulfills the purpose.

The release steward should require an explicit answer to: which loop, which moment, which action, what alternative, what evidence, and what would cause this feature to be changed or stopped? Its persistent role specification and decision log accompany this report. They provide continuity for later reviews; they are not an unattended monitoring service.
