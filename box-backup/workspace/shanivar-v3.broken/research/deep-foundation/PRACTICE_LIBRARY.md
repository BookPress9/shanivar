# Proposed experience library — research V1

Status: **PROPOSED**, 29 September 2026. These ten entries are original low-intensity design proposals for review and consented usability exploration. They are **not authorized public-release additions**, clinical interventions, traditional initiation or a replacement for the eight existing Steady scripts. Source fidelity, usability and benefit are separate questions.

All cue text below is original Shanivar writing. Ancient sources supply a comparison or inspiration only where explicitly stated. [Source ledger](SOURCE_LEDGER_ANCIENT.md) records access depth; [Tantra map](TANTRA_PRACTICE_MAP.md) records exclusions. A short duration limits the task; it does not establish safety for everyone.

## Shared experience contract

The person is stationary and chooses to participate. Eyes can remain open. No breath holds, pacing, force, bodily locks, dizziness targets or altered-state targets. An immediate **Stop** and **Try another anchor** remain available. Body/breath attention is optional; the room-orientation alternative is always available. No exercise is required to dismiss an alarm, contact help or regain essential access. Timer completion has no spiritual meaning.

For research, ask at most one optional immediate question: **“Did this feel useful, neutral, or uncomfortable?”** Allow no answer. Do not require autobiographical writing. Investigate reports of discomfort; do not classify them as growth. Do not infer a mental health condition, autonomic state or metaphysical attainment. New scripts need source/comfort/accessibility review before a public pilot.

The **Notice → Understand → Experience → Observe → Integrate** architecture is expressed as a small moment, not five mandatory screens. Often a single cue plus a next-action choice is enough. Explanation is optional and can come later.

## P01 — Feel one point of contact

- **Source and status:** modern Shanivar synthesis. A07's body-attention passages are a comparison, not the source of this exact contact exercise. No VBT verse attribution.
- **Moment / eligibility:** scattered, between tasks, or breath attention unwelcome; only if noticing contact feels comfortable. 10–20 seconds, seated or otherwise supported.
- **Original cue:** “Notice one place you meet the chair or floor. Feel the pressure there. Nothing needs to change.”
- **Observe → integrate:** “Was anything noticeable?” optional; then return to the task the person chose. A room object replaces body contact immediately if preferred.
- **Confidence:** high that wording is an original non-manipulative instruction; efficacy untested. **Principle:** start with an available sensation. **Product implication:** contact can be offered without spiritual vocabulary or a mood quiz.
- **Experiment:** compare contact-first and explanation-first introductions with people who decline breath attention. **Success:** they understand the action and can leave the app voluntarily. **Failure:** confusion, increased body vigilance, or feeling compelled to persist. **Decision:** proposed test after review; no release acceptance.

## P02 — Notice the room

- **Source and status:** modern synthesis, aligns with the existing `external_grounding` design; no ancient attribution.
- **Moment / eligibility:** unsure what to choose, overstimulated by inward attention; 10–20 seconds in a safe stationary setting. Adapt visually described objects to ordinary audible/tactile environmental information when needed.
- **Original cue:** “Let your eyes rest on an ordinary object nearby. Notice one detail. You can leave it at that.”
- **Observe → integrate:** no relaxation test; offer “Continue with what I was doing.” If looking around is uncomfortable, simply end.
- **Confidence:** usability hypothesis only. **Principle:** inward attention is not universally appropriate. **Product implication:** uncertainty routes to a choice, never an intensity escalation.
- **Experiment:** test discovery of the alternative from every practice. **Success:** users can switch with one clear action and no explanation. **Failure:** they believe the alternative is a lesser practice, or cannot use the visual cue. **Decision:** proposed accessibility/comfort test.

## P03 — Let a breath happen

- **Source and status:** modern introductory adaptation adjacent to VBT 24 (A12–13); not a literal translation. Omits subtle centers and retention. Buddhist MN 118 (A07) is a separate comparison, not fused provenance.
- **Moment / eligibility:** before chosen focus or an ordinary transition; breath attention explicitly welcome. 15–30 seconds. No schedule-based biological claim.
- **Original cue:** “Let your breathing find its own pace. Notice one breath, wherever it is easiest to feel. If that is not comfortable, look around the room instead.”
- **Observe → integrate:** no breath count or depth rating; start the chosen focus task or leave.
- **Confidence:** high for adaptation disclosure, low for benefit/moment matching. **Principle:** observe without controlling. **Product implication:** no animation that silently imposes a breathing rhythm.
- **Experiment:** ask users what the cue required. **Success:** they correctly report no requirement to breathe deeply or hold. **Failure:** they force, count or compete with the visual. **Decision:** test text with content review; preserve existing script version until accepted.

## P04 — Hear the ending

- **Source and status:** modern sound-attention adaptation inspired by VBT 41, A14. The optional sound is an original app asset; no traditional audio claim.
- **Moment / eligibility:** a short transition when sound is welcome and hearing the cue is practical. 15–30 seconds; not suitable when sound is unwelcome or the environment requires attention elsewhere.
- **Original cue:** “Listen to this soft sound as it fades. When it ends, let the ordinary sounds around you return.”
- **Observe → integrate:** no search for a hidden sound; no obligation to hold breath or keep listening after discomfort. Offer a silent contact alternative.
- **Confidence:** high for broad source relationship; specific asset and duration untested. **Principle:** one sensory event can anchor a clear beginning and end. **Product implication:** consent precedes playback and stop is immediate.
- **Experiment:** test volume control, hearing differences and interruption behavior. **Success:** users understand the endpoint and can return to activity. **Failure:** startling audio, straining to hear, tinnitus-related discomfort or unwanted repeat playback. **Decision:** proposed asset/usability test, not treatment.

## P05 — Sensation, then story

- **Source and status:** original synthesis with conceptual comparison to MN 18's *papañca* account, A08. This is not a translation of its causal sequence or a claim that all thinking is harmful.
- **Moment / eligibility:** an ordinary minor irritation or checking impulse; not an invitation to revisit traumatic material. 20–40 seconds. External alternative available.
- **Original cue:** “Notice one thing happening now: a sound, a touch, a movement. Is there also a story about it? You do not have to finish that story right now.”
- **Observe → integrate:** choose to continue the task, think deliberately about the issue, or take no action. Useful planning is allowed.
- **Confidence:** moderate conceptual link; effect unknown. **Principle:** distinguish observation from interpretation without dismissing either. **Product implication:** no app-generated explanation of what the story “really means.”
- **Experiment:** compare prompt comprehension across different language backgrounds. **Success:** participants can name a present event and an interpretation without judging themselves. **Failure:** they dismiss a real problem, become more entangled, or read the prompt as thought suppression. **Decision:** proposed wording test; revise on either failure.

## P06 — A small feeling, without a verdict

- **Source and status:** modern adaptation related to existing VBT v.101 content (A12 and prior catalogue). No promise of transforming anger into realization.
- **Moment / eligibility:** mild everyday frustration that the user voluntarily chooses to notice. 15–30 seconds. Do not select automatically from sentiment, birth data or inferred distress.
- **Original cue:** “If it feels manageable, notice where this small frustration shows up. You do not need to explain or intensify it. You can return to the room at any time.”
- **Observe → integrate:** invite one practical choice such as pausing a reply; no required emotional labeling or confession.
- **Confidence:** moderate source/adaptation relationship; comfort and benefit need review. **Principle:** agency includes choosing not to investigate. **Product implication:** no emotional exposure ladder.
- **Experiment:** review the cue first, then use only self-selected mild scenarios in usability work. **Success:** users understand permission to stop and choose an ordinary next action. **Failure:** recalled intense distress, pressure to endure, or escalation. **Decision:** hold public testing until appropriately scoped comfort review.

## P07 — Return once

- **Source and status:** original modern micro-practice informed by A02's *abhyāsa* discussion. Not a full Pātañjala method and no claim of samādhi.
- **Moment / eligibility:** before work/study, any comfortable anchor; 20–30 seconds.
- **Original cue:** “Choose one nearby detail to notice. If attention moves elsewhere, see whether you can return once. That return is enough for this moment.”
- **Observe → integrate:** immediately resume a user-chosen task. Do not count wanderings or display failure rates.
- **Confidence:** high conceptual attribution, low exact-script evidence. **Principle:** discipline is a repeatable choice. **Product implication:** use this as a candidate Shani expression without moralizing distraction.
- **Experiment:** compare with a completion/performance frame. **Success:** less self-blame and a clear chosen return, assessed by participant descriptions. **Failure:** users monitor for thoughts or try to achieve a blank mind. **Decision:** test; reject competitive scoring.

## P08 — Pleasant, unpleasant, or neither

- **Source and status:** simplified modern observation inspired by the *vedanā* section of MN 10, A07. It does not represent the full four establishments or an exhaustive emotion taxonomy.
- **Moment / eligibility:** ordinary sensation with low emotional intensity; 15–30 seconds. “Unsure” and skipping are valid.
- **Original cue:** “Notice one ordinary sensation. Does it feel pleasant, unpleasant, or neither? You can also leave it unnamed.”
- **Observe → integrate:** optionally notice whether there is an urge to keep or change it; avoid a second question if it adds friction. Return to the chosen activity.
- **Confidence:** high broad textual relation, low exact app effect. **Principle:** observation can precede reaction without replacing action. **Product implication:** an optional lightweight label, not a daily mood diagnosis.
- **Experiment:** ask participants to distinguish feeling-tone from a detailed emotional identity. **Success:** they can use or decline the label easily. **Failure:** compulsive checking, confusion or a belief that unpleasant means spiritually wrong. **Decision:** proposed limited test after translation review.

## P09 — Let the day end

- **Source and status:** modern adaptation adjacent to VBT 75 and existing `sleep_threshold`, A12. Omits any goal of maintaining unusual awareness; not insomnia treatment.
- **Moment / eligibility:** explicitly choosing sleep and already sleepy; includes daytime sleep. 20–40 seconds of optional cues, then silence. No clock-only trigger.
- **Original cue:** “You do not need to complete anything here. Notice the support beneath you, if that is comfortable. Let this be the last instruction.”
- **Observe → integrate:** screen may sleep; no completion question, bell, streak or demand to remain awake. End without penalty whenever wanted.
- **Confidence:** moderate adaptation relationship; sleep benefit untested. **Principle:** support the user's intent without requiring engagement. **Product implication:** silence is an endpoint, not abandonment.
- **Experiment:** test instructions and screen-off behavior before any sleep-outcome study. **Success:** users understand there is nothing to finish. **Failure:** they stay awake to achieve a score, wait for a sound, or feel commanded to sleep. **Decision:** proposed UX test; do not claim improved sleep efficacy.

## P10 — Choose the next small action

- **Source and status:** modern Shanivar integration synthesis; not a verse-derived ancient practice. Connects founder's mission to action rather than in-app dependency.
- **Moment / eligibility:** after any voluntarily completed observation, including stopping early; 5–15 seconds, no timer required.
- **Original cue:** “What is one small thing you choose to do next? You can put the phone down without answering here.”
- **Observe → integrate:** a private mental choice is sufficient. Optionally start existing focus or leave; never force a task entry or share card.
- **Confidence:** original product hypothesis. **Principle:** practice serves lived agency. **Product implication:** leaving is an intended success path, not churn to prevent.
- **Experiment:** compare optional integration with a next-session recommendation. **Success:** participants report an intentional offline action and need fewer cues over time. **Failure:** obligation, decision paralysis or an endless practice loop. **Decision:** test with founder acceptance; reject forced reflection.

## Explicit exclusions and release gates

Research may document the following but cannot automatically convert them to beginner experiences: counted holds; rapid/forceful breathing; respiratory cessation; energy ascent; bodily seals/locks; sensory occlusion; sexual/partner practices; intoxicants; induced fear or exhaustion; sleep deprivation; derealization targets; AI initiation; diagnostic chakra/guṇa readings; and attainment certification. An advanced study preference does not authorize advanced practice.

Before an entry becomes accepted: named source reviewer confirms attribution or modern-synthesis label; editor checks adaptation differences; appropriately qualified reviewer evaluates foreseeable comfort risks for the exact audience; accessibility review verifies alternatives and controls; user study checks comprehension and voluntary exit; founder records acceptance/version/date. No current row satisfies these gates merely by appearing in this file.

Do not measure benefit by session length, intensity, physiological conjecture or public sharing. Proposed primary observations are comprehension, usable choice, voluntary return to activity and reduced need for the app. Record neutral and negative responses with the same seriousness as positive ones. Pilot numbers and pass thresholds must be set in the actual study protocol, not invented after results.
