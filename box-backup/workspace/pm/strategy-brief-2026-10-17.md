SHANIVAR — Product Strategy & Execution Brief (from founder Shubham Kumar, 17 Oct 2026, verbatim)

Owner: Product Manager. Priority: Strategic alignment and execution readiness. Scope: Product, Frontend, Backend, QA/Testing, Marketing, Finance, Customer Success. Status: Approved strategic direction; implementation details require technical validation.

1. Executive directive
Shanivar is evolving from a collection of digital wellbeing features into an integrated system designed to help users move from automatic behavior to intentional action.
Competitive research across Opal, one sec, Freedom, Alarmy, Routinery, Headspace, Calm, Waking Up and Insight Timer suggests an opportunity to connect three functions:
1. Protection: Interrupt unwanted digital behavior and create space for a deliberate choice.
2. Routine: Turn intentions into small, repeatable physical and behavioral actions.
3. Awareness / Sadhana: Help users observe thoughts, moods and urges, then apply that understanding in daily life.
Approved tagline: Protect. Become Aware. Enter Flow.
Product thesis: Shanivar helps people reduce automatic digital behavior, build consistent routines and develop awareness through small, actionable practices.
The product must not optimize merely for screen time blocked, alarms dismissed, meditation minutes or app opens. It must help users make more intentional choices and recover when their routines break.
Long-term ambition: help users develop greater self-regulation and enter a state of focused, intentional engagement with life. Treat this as the product thesis to validate through user behavior and research, not an outcome already proven.

2. Immediate responsibilities of the PM
A. Update Notion as source of truth. Canonical page titled "Shanivar Product Thesis — Protection, Awareness & Flow" with sections: approved tagline and thesis; competitive landscape and research findings; target users and primary problems; core product principles and non-goals; proposed end-to-end user journey; Customer Success outcome framework; product roadmap and prioritization; technical dependencies and platform limitations; risks, assumptions and open questions; links to all implementation, testing and launch tickets; decision log, owner, status and last-updated date. Preserve existing product decisions and link the thesis to the current roadmap. Do not overwrite earlier decisions without recording what changed and why.
B. Traceable execution plan: User problem -> Product hypothesis -> Requirement -> Implementation ticket -> Test case -> Measured outcome. Each ticket: owner, priority, acceptance criteria, dependencies, test coverage, clear definition of done. Do not convert strategic assumptions into unquestioned requirements; label unvalidated hypotheses and how they will be tested.
C. Cross-functional alignment review: share a concise thesis with each team; each returns: What must change? What already exists? What is missing? Dependencies/risks? What can ship in first release? How will the result be verified? Resolve conflicting interpretations before development begins.

3. Frontend brief. Objective: make the transition from protection to intentional action intuitive, reliable, low-friction.
Work: 1 review Routine, Sadhana and protection UIs against the thesis; 2 map journey from selected goal through intervention, action, reflection; 3 MVP experience that does not overwhelm first-time users; 4 short optional awareness prompt on distraction or relevant action completion; 5 clear routine progress, missed-step recovery, non-punitive return; 6 loading, empty, error, permission-denied, offline states; 7 accessible controls, readable typography, predictable navigation; 8 instrument agreed analytics events without unnecessary sensitive info.
Deliverables: updated user-flow diagrams; wireframes/interface specs; component and state specs; implementation tickets linked to backend deps; frontend test cases and a11y checks.
AC: new user can identify and complete first meaningful action without configuring the whole product; user understands why an intervention appeared and the choices; a missed action never creates an endless loop or unrecoverable state; errors communicated clearly, failed requests never falsely show success; analytics fire consistently, not duplicated by rerenders; all core flows pass the QA suite.

4. Backend brief. Objective: dependable, secure, measurable foundation for the behavior-change loop.
Work: 1 audit existing APIs, tables, auth, analytics before proposing infra; 2 data models and APIs for routines, protection sessions, practice completions, reflections, progress; 3 consistent event schema and timestamps; 4 authorization and per-user data isolation for every endpoint; 5 server-side validation, rate limits, error handling, idempotency; 6 routine completion and reward logic cannot be duplicated by repeat requests; 7 scheduled messages, reminders, practice delivery with retry and observable failure; 8 document retention, deletion, backup, recovery; 9 review third-party integrations, secrets, logging, production config.
Deliverables: architecture and data-flow updates; API specs and migrations; security and privacy review; event dictionary and analytics contract; automated unit and integration tests; deployment, rollback, monitoring instructions.
AC: users cannot access/modify others' records; authn, authz, input validation tested; repeated requests cannot duplicate completions or rewards; failed operations observable and recoverable; sensitive data/credentials not exposed in responses, analytics or logs; DB changes migration-controlled, tested, reversible where feasible; every new endpoint documents success, validation and failure behavior.

5. QA brief. Objective: independently verify Shanivar across normal, failure and recovery scenarios; never rely solely on developer confirmation.
Work: 1 every AC into a reproducible test case; 2 test onboarding and full protection->action->reflection flow; 3 routine creation, completion, interruption, missed steps, recovery; 4 permission denial, notifications, background, restarts, connectivity loss, API failures; 5 validate analytics vs actual actions; 6 authz boundaries, sessions, input validation, duplicate requests; 7 supported device/OS behavior and platform limits; 8 regression before every RC; 9 defects with severity, evidence, expected, actual.
Deliverables: test plan linked to requirements; test cases and results; defect register; regression report; release recommendation and outstanding-risk summary.
Definition of done: not complete because it appears on screen; complete when ACs pass, integration and regression pass, analytics verified, no unresolved critical/high defect without explicit documented approval. Alarm and protection features: verify on real devices and OS constraints, not only simulators.

6. Customer Success brief. Objective: determine whether users achieve the intended outcome and why they struggle, disengage or return.
Work: 1 activation milestone per primary segment; 2 baseline activation, W1 and W4 retention; 3 track meaningful actions, not only opens/time; 4 segment by primary goal: digital distraction, routine consistency, meditation, philosophical self-inquiry; 5 interviews with active, inactive, returning users; 6 abandonment reasons: technical failure, excessive friction, low relevance, unrealistic routines, unclear value, other; 7 lapsed-user recovery journey offering smaller commitments, no guilt or pressure; 8 recurring evidence-based findings to Product and Marketing.
North Star: Weekly Intentional Actions Completed (WIAC) — distinct occasions users complete an action they deliberately selected through Shanivar, deduplicated across features. Alongside: activation rate; time to first value; routine completion rate; protection-window effectiveness; meditation return rate; W1/W4 retention; recovery after missed days; self-reported intentionality and usefulness; technical reliability.
Deliverables: CS measurement plan; segment definitions; interview guide and feedback taxonomy; baseline dashboard; monthly product-insights report; recommendations linked to tickets. Do not treat higher engagement as proof of improved awareness; pair behavioral metrics with self-reported outcomes and state measurement limits.

7. Marketing brief. Objective: communicate the approved promise accurately and attract users whose problems Shanivar addresses.
Work: 1 messaging around "Protect. Become Aware. Enter Flow."; 2 explain protection, routine and awareness relationship plainly; 3 differentiate on the integrated experience, not unsupported claims competitors lack features; 4 separate messaging for distraction, inconsistent routines, meditation adherence; 5 test understanding of the promise before larger campaigns; 6 align website, onboarding, social, launch comms; 7 no medical, therapeutic or guaranteed-performance claims; 8 promises match actual feature availability and platform limits.
Deliverables: positioning and messaging doc; value proposition and segments; landing-page and onboarding copy; messaging experiments and reporting plan; feature-claim verification checklist. Measure by share of acquired users who activate and reach their first goal, not only clicks/installs.

8. Finance brief. Objective: financial viability without monetization undermining trust.
Work: 1 model infra, third-party API, messaging, maintenance, support costs; 2 cost per activated and per retained user; 3 subscription or alternative monetization vs perceived value; 4 CAC, conversion, retention, revenue scenarios with explicit assumptions; 5 cost risks from AI content, notifications, usage growth; 6 pricing experiments and free vs paid boundaries; 7 ensure essential controls and privacy are not weakened for conversion.
Deliverables: unit-economics model; monthly opex forecast; pricing and monetization scenarios; assumption register; financial dashboard aligned with activation and retention. Do not price from competitors alone; validate willingness to pay with target users.

9. Integrated backlog: an epic per workstream; link all implementation and QA tickets to the thesis.
Epic A Strategy and measurement: Notion thesis; map existing features vs journey; event taxonomy and North Star; baseline and experiment design; assumptions and decision log.
Epic B Protection and intentional choice: audit protection capabilities and platform constraints; define protection-window behavior and controls; implement intervention and choice recording; reliability monitoring and failure handling; test permissions, sessions, recovery.
Epic C Routine and action execution: routine data model and completion semantics; routine setup and first-action flow; missed-step recovery; verify alarm and notification behavior on supported devices; routine analytics.
Epic D Sadhana and awareness: initial set of short practices; map each to a user need and expected action; optional reflection and practice completion; connect relevant Mano Vijnana content to practical exercises; validate content clarity, autonomy, analytics.
Epic E Security, privacy, reliability: review authn/authz; validate access controls and isolation; review sensitive logging and retention; test duplicates, errors, migrations; release, rollback, incident-response docs.
Epic F QA and release readiness: traceable test cases per epic; E2E and regression; verify analytics and dashboards; defects and retests; release-readiness report.
Epic G Customer Success and growth: activation and retention dashboards; feedback and churn-reason taxonomy; lapsed-user recovery journey; marketing positioning and acquisition experiments; recurring cross-functional product review.
Prioritize foundational work, reliability, security and the first end-to-end journey before expanding features.

10. Mandatory ticket fields: Title (specific, action-oriented); Epic; User problem; Hypothesis; Scope (in and explicitly out); Requirements (functional and non-functional); Acceptance criteria (observable, testable); Dependencies; Analytics (events, properties, expected behavior); Security/privacy; Test cases (normal, edge, failure, recovery); Owner and priority; Definition of done (code review, tests, docs, telemetry, release); Evidence (links to design, implementation, test results, decisions). Independently actionable; no vague "improve UX" or "make app secure".

11. Release gates: G1 Product alignment (documented user problem, supports thesis); G2 Design and technical review (flow, architecture, deps, platform constraints documented); G3 Implementation (code review, error handling, analytics, docs complete); G4 Independent testing (ACs, regression, security tests pass); G5 CS readiness (can measure outcome, identify failure patterns, explain feature); G6 Release approval (risks documented, rollback understood, release owner approves). Any unresolved critical security, data-isolation or critical reliability defect blocks release.

12. Required PM outputs: 1 canonical Notion thesis; 2 roadmap with priorities and dependencies; 3 short delegation brief per team; 4 structured epic and ticket backlog for FE and BE; 5 QA test cases linked to ACs; 6 measurement plan shared with CS, Product, Marketing; 7 finance model request with documented assumptions; 8 cross-functional decision log; 9 release-readiness checklist and reporting cadence.
Operating principle: PM owns alignment and traceability; each team owns execution; tester independently verifies; CS validates meaningful value. No team implements a major interpretation of the thesis without recording the decision in Notion.
Final directive: Do not build more features simply because research identifies them. Identify the user problem, define the smallest useful intervention, test it, measure it, improve from evidence. Central promise: Protect. Become Aware. Enter Flow. The product succeeds when users need less intervention to act intentionally, not when they become more dependent on the app.
