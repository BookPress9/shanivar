# Practice safety and claim discipline

Status: PROPOSED product policy, 2026-09-29; not a clinical clearance or comprehensive contraindication manual. Applies to research-to-product translation, not a diagnosis of any user. Source methods and access limits: [psychology ledger](SOURCE_LEDGER_PSYCHOLOGY.md). The existing Steady scope already excludes prescribed retention and provides external-attention alternatives; this research does not authorize replacing those safeguards with advanced practice.

## Evidence and its proper use

Meditation-related unwanted effects are documented, while reporting and definitions remain inconsistent. [Farias et al., P08](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32820538/) support taking harms seriously, not assigning an individual risk percentage. [Lindahl et al., P09](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0176239) map challenging experiences through a deliberately selected sample; their sample's frequency cannot estimate beginner-app prevalence. [NCCIH, P18](https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/meditation-and-mindfulness-effectiveness-and-safety) emphasizes limited safety research and not replacing conventional care.

The product inference is to provide choice, easy stopping and honest limits. It is not to tell every visitor that ordinary attention is dangerous, nor to promise that a short practice is universally safe.

## Categories and disposition

| Practice category | Context and evidence boundary | Proposed product disposition / necessary review |
|---|---|---|
| Ordinary external attention, gentle contact awareness | Lower-demand design choice; not a guarantee for all people/settings | Beginner candidate with eyes open, ordinary breathing, no target feeling, skip/stop and accessible alternatives |
| Natural breath observation | Awareness differs from respiratory manipulation; comfort varies | Optional only; no “breathe perfectly,” mandatory nasal breathing or fixed depth. External anchor stays equally valid |
| Intensive breath manipulation / hyperventilation | Clinical/research protocol findings cannot generalize to unselected users; [NCCIH yoga guidance, P19](https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/yoga-effectiveness-and-safety) cautions beginners about forceful breathing | Research catalogue only. Qualified breath/clinical review would be required for any future proposal; no automated activation/energy booster |
| Prolonged retention / breath-hold competitions | Context-specific hypoxia risk appears in [P21](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15778900/); traditional inclusion supplies no app safety dose | Exclude from beginner scripts and progression. No breath scores, delayed permission to inhale, maximum-hold goals or aquatic use |
| Fasting / food or fluid restriction | Health/medication interactions are material; [NIDDK P20](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/professionals/diabetes-discoveries-practice/fasting-safely-with-diabetes) illustrates why individual context matters | Historical context only. No dietary prescriptions, medication changes or fasting streaks; future content needs qualified nutrition/clinical and tradition review |
| Sleep restriction / overnight vigil | [P17](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30042701/) concerns prolonged deprivation and cannot define a safe challenge | Exclude sleep-loss goals, wakefulness competitions and “advanced” overnight unlocks. Bedtime practice must permit falling asleep and ending quietly |
| Extreme concentration / sensory deprivation | Challenge reports in P09 cannot specify a safe app threshold or universal readiness measure | No escalation based on minutes/streaks. Intensive retreat or deprivation instruction stays outside unattended product |
| Strong somatic techniques | Movement, forced postures and pressure can require individual modification; broad yoga evidence is not technique-specific | No shaking/catharsis/pressure claims as trauma release. Qualified review for any strong movement protocol; accessible ordinary contact alternative |
| Kundalini-oriented practice | Traditional vocabulary and unusual experiences have differing interpretations; no validated app detector or physiological score here | Contextual study may be offered, labelled by tradition. No “awakening” diagnosis, energy-unblocking promise or algorithmic induction |
| Altered-state techniques | Intensity or unusual perception does not establish benefit or realization; challenging experiences have multiple explanations (P09) | Do not reward intensity, perceptual changes or loss of ordinary functioning. No drug/psychedelic protocols or automated state escalation |
| Sexuality-related tantric material | Lineage, consent, power, adulthood and context are indispensable; no app-safety trial reviewed here | Scholarly catalogue only for this initiative. No partner instruction, arousal-based prescription or reinterpretation of sexuality as all of Tantra; specialist cultural/consent review before future scope |
| Trauma-sensitive practices | Professional clinical intervention evidence such as [P22](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38064219/) cannot certify a generic unattended body prompt | Do not request trauma disclosure or ask users to revisit memories. Offer external attention, movement/change of position when safe, eyes open and immediate exit. No treatment claim |

These are scope decisions under uncertainty. “Requires qualified guidance” does not mean every teacher or branded certificate provides adequate safeguarding, and guidance cannot be replaced by a checkbox disclaimer.

## User control and escalation design

Before a practice, establish a suitable stationary setting through simple user choice; do not interrupt driving, cycling, swimming or operation of equipment. No sensor-based assertion that the user is safe. Avoid unnecessary medical intake for ordinary attention practices; request only information that changes a current choice.

During a session, keep End reachable without explanation, penalty or confirmation maze. Allow normal breathing at all times; a timer measures session time, not breath compliance. If discomfort arises, the user can stop, look around or choose an external anchor. Do not instruct persistence through dizziness, panic, unreality or pain as spiritual progress.

For a voluntarily reported persistent or severe unwanted experience, stop the practice recommendation and suggest appropriate qualified support. Severe physical symptoms, immediate danger or inability to stay safe need local urgent help rather than another exercise. This is a proposed boundary for reviewed support copy, not an AI triage system. Never infer suicidality, diagnosis or readiness from app usage or a feeling button.

## Review and testing contract

- **Source review:** verify what the tradition actually prescribes, what is omitted and what Shanivar added. Retention omitted from a traditional passage must be disclosed as adaptation.
- **Practice review:** a suitably qualified human reviews each exact script, context, alternatives and claims before public release; citation verification alone is insufficient.
- **Interaction review:** confirm stop/skip visibility, accessibility, sound consent and source access; no practice can satisfy alarm dismissal or release a focus lock.
- **Content checks:** no instruction to suppress breath, exhaust air, force a sensation, obey an interpretation, lose sleep or earn spiritual status. Pattern matching helps find errors but does not replace reading.
- **Pilot feedback:** ask optionally whether anything felt uncomfortable or unhelpful; record no response separately. Collect minimum necessary information, not private trauma narratives or detailed mental-health profiles.
- **Adverse feedback response:** preserve consented minimal evidence, stop the affected recommendation, review before reuse and inform the relevant owner. Do not silently treat an early exit as poor adherence.

No safety test should ask participants to endure an unsafe practice. Test exclusion rules with fixtures and scripted scenarios. Physical-device checks establish technical behavior, not clinical safety or efficacy.

## Claims to reject now

“Clinically proven VBT,” “reset your nervous system,” “cure brain rot,” “unlock kundalini,” “heal trauma in one minute,” “your emotions reveal your dosha/chart,” and “engineered to your cortisol cycle” exceed the evidence reviewed. Prefer an invitation: “Try a brief practice. Notice whether it helps.” Source details can explain traditional context and modern adaptation without making the user read a safety or philosophy essay before a simple, voluntary choice.
