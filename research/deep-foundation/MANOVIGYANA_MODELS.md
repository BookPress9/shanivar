# Manovigyana: models of ordinary mental experience

Status: PROPOSED research synthesis, 2026-09-29. Owner: Manovigyana / Human Psychology Researcher. “Manovigyana” here names Shanivar's inquiry into mind; it is not claimed as one ancient school or a validated diagnostic taxonomy. Traditional meanings remain in the [ancient knowledge map](ANCIENT_KNOWLEDGE_MAP.md). Modern sources, methods and access limits are in the [psychology ledger](SOURCE_LEDGER_PSYCHOLOGY.md).

The product question is not how to eliminate thought. It is whether a person can notice what is happening and choose an appropriate next action. Thinking may be useful planning, imagination, reflection or problem-solving. The [Watkins review, P01](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18298268/) distinguishes constructive from unconstructive repetitive thinking by content, context and processing; frequency alone is inadequate.

## Experience-to-intervention map

These are design hypotheses, not diagnoses inferred from taps. The user may reject the offered description. “I do not know” and direct access to a simple practice remain valid.

| Phenomenon | Working modern distinction and evidence | Smallest proposed product response | What could disprove usefulness |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rumination | Repeated analysis can become unproductive; repeated thought also serves planning (P01) | Ask “Is there one thing you can do next?”; optionally notice an external sensation | More self-analysis, shame or the same unresolved loop after the prompt |
| Automatic behavior | An action may be cued before deliberate reconsideration; not every automatic action is bad (P02/P12) | A user-chosen boundary offers a clear exit to the intended activity | Automatic dismissal of Shanivar or substitution with another feed |
| Attention switching | Laboratory switch costs depend on task structure; not a universal loss-of-IQ claim (P14) | Preserve the user's selected next task during an interruption | Prompt becomes another interruption with no useful return |
| Cognitive overload | Working-memory constraints motivate reducing simultaneous demands; no magic menu size follows (P16) | One meaningful next choice; show details on request | Fewer options hide a needed action and increase searching/backtracking |
| Compulsive checking | Self-described unwanted opening is not a clinical compulsion diagnosis (P02/P03) | Let user identify one recurring moment and selected distracting app | Necessary communication is obstructed or checking shifts devices |
| Avoidance | Suppressing a thought can rebound in experimental contexts (P15); leaving a practice can also be wise self-protection | “You can leave this for now”; offer concrete chosen action, not forced emotional confrontation | The app labels every skip as avoidance or makes the user endure distress |
| Emotional reactivity | Monitoring and acceptance are separable training components (P05) | Invite noticing without a demand to fix: “No need to change it right now” | User becomes more vigilant, judged or disconnected from a practical need |
| Interoception | Perception of internal signals, beliefs about them and attention to them are not interchangeable (P04) | Optional natural-breath awareness; external sound/contact alternative | User finds internal attention uncomfortable or assumes the app measured physiology |
| Body awareness | Multidimensional self-report differs from objective accuracy (P04) | Notice where a hand meets a surface; no target sensation required | “I cannot feel it” becomes failure or a score |
| Habit loops | Stable cues and repeated behavior are useful research models, with substantial variation (P12) | User-authored when–then plan with restart after a lapse | Streak protection replaces the activity or a rigid schedule becomes burdensome |
| Motivation | Values, pressure and intentions are distinguishable; wording alone does not ensure behavior change (P11) | Ask what the protected time is for, once and optionally | Guilt increases; intention is recorded but chosen activity does not occur |
| Resistance | Could mean poor fit, pressure, bad timing, accessibility or legitimate disagreement (P10/P11) | Respect “not now”; editable plan and transparent override | System escalates friction as punishment or calls disagreement low readiness |
| Curiosity | Trivia-learning evidence suggests situational curiosity can support memory, not spiritual progress (P13) | One relevant optional question; make source depth available after experience | Mystery becomes an endless content hook |
| Self-reflection | Constructive review requires context and a purpose (P01) | “Did this help you choose what to do next?”; optional answer | Reflection length rises while real-world follow-through falls |

## An explicit four-layer model

1. **Reported moment:** “I opened the app without meaning to.” This is the person's account, not a hidden state inferred by AI.
2. **Possible modern mechanism:** cue-driven behavior or avoidance is a hypothesis. Check alternative explanations such as urgent communication or habitually reaching for a tool.
3. **Traditional inquiry:** a named tradition may explore attention, attachment or identification. Its liberation framework and metaphysics are not replaced by the modern hypothesis.
4. **Shanivar experiment:** one optional observation and chosen action. Its outcome must be evaluated in its own right.

Do not rename these layers “survival,” “observer,” “architect,” or “cosmic consciousness” and rank users. A person can need a simple grounding choice after years of practice and choose textual study on their first visit. Duration, streaks and confident self-description cannot certify realization.

## Measurement and next work

Record only what is needed to learn: eligible moment, offered choice, choice made, technical result, optional account of what happened afterward. A completed timer does not prove attention. A closed target app does not prove wellbeing. Nonuse may mean independence, dissatisfaction, technical failure or an unknown reason.

Proposed experiment M1 compares a concrete next-action question with an otherwise identical generic reflection prompt. Recruit consenting adults with a recurring unwanted-checking moment; do not screen or label them as disordered. Keep enforcement, timing and duration constant. Primary signal: voluntary report of following the preselected activity after an eligible interruption, with all eligible episodes and nonresponses reported. Failure: more reflection time, judgment, unwanted disclosure or lost access to necessary communication. Begin with comprehension interviews, then a predefined feasibility window; this is not an efficacy trial. Decision: TEST after current device/safety gates, not automatic adoption.

Unresolved: which situations benefit from internal versus external attention; whether a pause adds value beyond a dismissal option; what allows practice transfer outside the app; which forms of reflection increase rather than reduce mental elaboration. These need actual audience evidence, including negative cases and nonusers.
