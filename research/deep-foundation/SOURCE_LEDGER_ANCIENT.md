Status: **PROPOSED**. Researched 29 September 2026. Owner: Ancient Knowledge / Textual Provenance workstream. Founder acceptance: pending. This ledger documents what was accessed, not credentials, initiation, clinical validation or permission to reproduce a translation.

Read alongside [knowledge map](ANCIENT_KNOWLEDGE_MAP.md), [separation matrix](TRADITION_SEPARATION_MATRIX.md), [Tantra map](TANTRA_PRACTICE_MAP.md) and [proposed experiences](PRACTICE_LIBRARY.md). Each source ID is stable across these files. All unattributed product language is original Shanivar synthesis, never an ancient quotation.

## Access and confidence rules

**P** = primary text in the identified edition/translation; **C** = commentary; **S** = modern scholarship; **M** = modern practitioner interpretation. Access depth: **selected passage**, **article/selected sections**, **abstract/catalogue only**, or **prior project audit**. A website carrying the whole book does not mean the whole book was read. Dates concern composition/redaction, not surviving manuscript age. High confidence means the cited source supports the bounded attribution; it does not certify that its metaphysics is true. Hypotheses about app benefit remain unvalidated.

## Teaching records

### A01 — Distinguishing cognitive functions

- P: Īśvarakṛṣṇa, *Sāṃkhyakārikā* 3, 12–13, 17–20, 23–27, 33; classical Sāṃkhya, approximately fourth century CE, exact date uncertain. Terms: *puruṣa, prakṛti, buddhi, ahaṃkāra, manas, guṇa*.
- Edition: [GRETIL Sanskrit, Ferenc Ruzsa input, 2020 HTML conversion](https://gretil.sub.uni-goettingen.de/gretil/corpustei/transformations/html/sa_IzvarakRSNa-sAMkhyakArikA.htm); its header identifies multiple collated editions. No new independent Sanskrit translation is claimed. Access: selected passages/header.
- S/interpretive triangulation: Ferenc Ruzsa, [Sāṃkhya, IEP](https://iep.utm.edu/sankhya/), sections on history and epistemology/psychology; article sections read. Traditional commentary: *Yuktidīpikā* and Vācaspati Miśra's *Tattvakaumudī* identified by the source, not independently read here.
- Context/confidence: high for distinguishing mind-functions from consciousness in this system; moderate chronology. Disagreement: earlier Sāṃkhya formations differ from the surviving Kārikā system. Relevance: comparison cards may distinguish sensation, interpretation and ownership; no app personality test based on guṇas.

### A02 — Pātañjala practice and mental activity

- P/C loci: *Yogasūtra* 1.2–4, 1.12–16, 1.33, 2.3, 2.29–32, 2.49–53, 3.1–3; classical Yoga. Terms: *citta-vṛtti, nirodha, abhyāsa, vairāgya, kleśa, dhāraṇā*. Approximately fourth–fifth century CE for the compiled *Pātañjalayogaśāstra* in a significant modern reconstruction; dating/authorship disputed.
- Access: [IIT Kanpur Sanskrit 1.3 with Bhāṣya and Bhoja](https://www.gitasupersite.iitk.ac.in/yogasutra_content?enable_bhaysa=1&enable_sutra=1&enable_vritti=1&field_chapter_value=1&field_nsutra_value=3&language=dv), selected passage; remaining loci checked through Edwin Bryant's [IEP scholarly exposition](https://iep.utm.edu/yoga/), not a complete primary-edition read. Bryant is the expositor here; do not label the IIT text a Bryant translation.
- S: Jason Birch, [Patañjali and Vyāsa](https://www.theluminescent.org/2017/06/patanjali-and-vyasa.html), 2017, article read, discusses Philipp Maas's textual research. Traditional attribution separates Patañjali and Vyāsa; the author-redactor hypothesis treats sūtra and bhāṣya as a unified composition. Hold both descriptions explicitly.
- Confidence high for the named doctrinal distinctions, moderate for historical reconstruction. *Nirodha* is not adequately translated into an app command to suppress unwanted thoughts. Product relevance: return without punishment; never describe a short timer as samādhi or kaivalya.

### A03 — Upaniṣadic diversity and the chariot

- P: *Kaṭha Upaniṣad* 1.3.3–13; terms *ātman, buddhi, manas, indriya*. Late first millennium BCE is a broad working placement; relative chronology remains debated.
- Translation: F. Max Müller, *The Upanishads*, Part II, Sacred Books of the East 15 (1884), [third vallī](https://sacred-texts.com/hin/sbe15/sbe15012.htm), selected passage and notes read. The host's generic series date can misleadingly show 1879; Part II is 1884. Colonial-era wording is not the app's voice. Śaṅkara's interpretation is reported in Müller's notes, not independently collated.
- S: Brian Black, [Upaniṣads, IEP](https://iep.utm.edu/upanisad/), selected historical/self sections read. Early prose *Bṛhadāraṇyaka* and *Chāndogya* are generally placed earlier than *Kaṭha*; the entire corpus has no single date or single doctrine.
- Confidence high for the chariot attribution, moderate dating; later Vedāntas read these texts differently. Relevance: a historical metaphor for steering attention, not a nervous-system diagram or proof of a hidden controller.

### A04 — Advaita is a specific reading

- Tradition/loci: Śaṅkara, eighth century CE, *Brahmasūtrabhāṣya* opening superimposition discussion and Upaniṣadic commentaries; *adhyāsa, ātman, brahman, avidyā*. P/C texts identified through scholarship, not fully read.
- S: Neil Dalal, [Śaṅkara, Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/shankara/), 2021/current web edition, selected doctrine/method sections read. Recognized further translation: Sengaku Mayeda, *A Thousand Teachings*, 1992 edition, **not read in this audit**.
- Confidence high that Advaita identifies ātman and brahman; internal and cross-school debates remain. Product relevance: optional attributed study, never the app diagnosing the user's true self.

### A05 — Viśiṣṭādvaita

- Tradition/loci: Rāmānuja, eleventh–twelfth century, *Vedārthasaṅgraha* and *Śrībhāṣya* 1.1.1; *śarīra/śarīrin, brahman, bhakti*. P/C passages accessed **through scholarship**, not an independently read translation.
- S: Shyam Ranganathan, [Rāmānuja, IEP](https://iep.utm.edu/ramanuja/), selected theism/metaphysics sections. Editorial translation/paraphrase is Ranganathan's; full edition comparison pending.
- Teaching: plurality and devotion retain significance within dependence on Brahman. Confidence high for the broad contrast with Advaita, moderate for compressed details; later Śrīvaiṣṇava developments must not be retrojected wholesale. Relevance: do not assume every seeker wants identity-with-the-absolute language.

### A06 — Dvaita and other Vedāntas

- Tradition: Madhva, thirteenth century; *bheda, Viṣṇu, jīva*. Primary locus: Madhva's Brahmasūtra commentarial corpus, **no individual verse audited**; no verse-level release attribution yet.
- S: Valerie Stoker, [Madhva, IEP](https://iep.utm.edu/madhva/), article read. Andrew J. Nicholson, [Bhedābheda Vedānta, IEP](https://iep.utm.edu/bhedabheda-vedanta/), selected introduction/history read, independently establishes a broader field than the familiar three schools. Primary/commentary editions not independently accessed.
- Confidence high that Dvaita maintains real difference and devotion; medium on broad survey generalization. Relevance: the app's comparative taxonomy cannot equate all Vedānta with nondualism. No doctrinal ranking.

### A07 — Breath and embodied recollection in early Buddhist texts

- P: *Ānāpānasati Sutta*, MN 118; *Satipaṭṭhāna Sutta*, MN 10. Pāli/Nikāya transmission, early Buddhist layers preserved through oral/redactional history; no exact discourse date asserted. Terms: *sati, sampajañña, kāya, vedanā, citta, dhamma*.
- Translation: Ṭhānissaro Bhikkhu, [MN 118](https://accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/mn/mn.118.than.html) and [MN 10](https://accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/mn/mn.010.than.html), current Access to Insight web versions; selected breath/body/feeling passages and notes read. [Sujato MN 118](https://suttacentral.net/mn118/en/sujato) located, but browser extraction returned only the shell: **not an independently read second translation**.
- Confidence high for source locations; interpretation of whole-body breath instruction and translation of *sati* are debated. Practice appears within liberation, ethics and training, not solely relaxation. Relevance: sensory options and noticing pleasant/unpleasant/neutral; not a claim that a 30-second adaptation delivers the full training.

### A08 — Proliferation and not-self are distinct topics

- P: [MN 18](https://accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/mn/mn.018.than.html), *Madhupiṇḍika*, Ṭhānissaro translation, sensory-contact/thinking passage and notes read; term *papañca*. [SN 22.59](https://accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/sn/sn22/sn22.059.nymo.html), *Anattalakkhaṇa*, Ñāṇamoli translation, aggregate/not-self argument read; *anattā, khandha*. Period: early discourse tradition, exact composition uncertain.
- Confidence high for textual argument, lower for equating *papañca* with contemporary rumination. MN 18's staged account is a contemplative explanation, not an experimentally established neural pipeline. Not-self is not permission to tell users they do not exist. Relevance: optional distinction between present stimulus and added story; retain ordinary responsibility and agency.

### A09 — Later Buddhist mind frameworks

- S: Noa Ronkin, [Abhidharma, SEP](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/abhidharma/); Szilvia Szanyi, [Yogācāra, SEP](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/yogacara/); [Madhyamaka, SEP](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/madhyamaka/), current web editions, selected taxonomy/history sections read. These are scholarly surveys, **not three primary texts read**.
- Loci for future primary checks: Vasubandhu, *Triṃśikā* 1–7; Nāgārjuna, *Mūlamadhyamakakārikā* 24.18; distinct Abhidharma corpora. Terms: *dharma, ālayavijñāna, kliṣṭamanas, śūnyatā, svabhāva*. Abhidharma develops over centuries around the turn of the era; major Yogācāra texts fourth–fifth centuries CE; Nāgārjuna around second–third centuries, all approximate.
- Confidence high for distinctions, medium for compressed chronology; Yogācāra's idealism and Madhyamaka's interpretation are contested. Relevance: rich optional study, never classify users into eight consciousness scores or equate emptiness with dissociation.

### A10 — Śaiva/Śākta institutions and plurality

- S: Alexis Sanderson, [The Śaiva Age](https://zenodo.org/records/4395629), in Einoo (ed.), *Genesis and Development of Tantrism*, University of Tokyo, 2009, pp. 41–350. PDF directly fetched; selected printed pp. 41–42, 45–49, 252–254 read. **Not the complete 310-page study.**
- Sources discussed include inscriptions and tantric corpora; *Atimārga, Mantramārga, Siddhānta, Trika, Krama, Kaula*. Focus fifth–thirteenth centuries. Confidence high for diversity, medium for general historical causality. The author's reconstruction is scholarship, not a tradition-neutral final history.
- Relevance: distinguish ritual systems and their settings; no universal secret tradition or single-cause disappearance narrative. Independent check: Maciej Karasiñski, [Śākta Tantra, Oxford Bibliographies](https://academic.oup.com/reference/62357/reference-article-abstract/571503296), published 21 February 2022, version 26 July 2022; introduction read only, not paywalled references.

### A11 — Recognition traditions

- S: Isabelle Ratié, *Utpaladeva and Abhinavagupta on the Freedom of Consciousness*, *Oxford Handbook of Indian Philosophy*, 2017, pp. 437–468. [Author's institutional bibliography](https://cv.hal.science/isabelle-ratie?langChosen=fr) verified; [HAL record](https://hal.science/halshs-01339661) blocked by bot check, OUP full chapter inaccessible. **Bibliographic/abstract depth only**.

### A12 — VBT base text and modern source distinction

- P/M: [Vijñāna Bhairava, Christopher Wallis's current web translation](https://tantra112.app/vijnana-bhairava-tantra). This audit revisited selected source passages; the [25 September project catalogue](../shanivar-vbt-breathing-catalog-2026-09-25.md) records the earlier complete English practice-section audit. Neither implies an independent Sanskrit edition or all commentaries read.
- Tradition: Śaiva tantric scripture, received in Kashmirian traditions; early medieval, exact composition/date uncertain. Do not print a confident founding year. Terms and practice references are inventoried in the linked catalogue.
- Confidence high for references in this web edition; medium interpretive fidelity; low for clinical benefit of adaptations. Jaideva Singh and Lilian Silburn are important independent edition comparisons **not completed here**. Modern Wallis explanations are not anonymous ancient instructions.

### A13 — VBT breath interpretation and contested passages

- P/C/M: Wallis [24–27 commentary](https://hareesh.org/blog/2022/11/30/vijana-bhairava-tantra-verses-22-27-the-foundational-practice), [55 commentary](https://hareesh.org/blog/2025/11/29/vijaana-bhairava-tantra-verse-55-freedom-in-the-dream-state), [closing verses](https://hareesh.org/blog/2025/9/30/the-closing-verses-of-the-vijana-bhairava-tantra), selected passages read. Terms *prāṇa, jīva, kumbhaka, mukta/svapna, haṃsa*.
- The first cites eighteenth-century Śivopādhyāya; that Sanskrit commentary was not independently read. Verse 55 variant changes interpretation; 156-A has manuscript qualifications. Grouping 24–27 as one *yukti* is Wallis's argued reading, not universal numbering.
- Confidence high that disagreements exist; medium adjudication. Relevance: preserve edition/verse strings and adaptation notes; beginner unpaced breathing explicitly omits source retention/energy details. No dream-control or physiologic claims.

### A14 — Sound as a specific sensory doorway

- P/M: VBT 40–41 in Wallis's [commentary](https://hareesh.org/blog/2024/12/7/vijaana-bhairava-tantra-verses-40-amp-41), selected text/explanation read. Terms *nāda* and contemplation of sound; source context is contemplative realization.
- Confidence high for the sound reference; no evidence here for tinnitus treatment or nervous-system resetting. Relevance: optional original fading tone, never force users to hear an internal sound. A 20-second UI listening experiment is Shanivar's adaptation.

### A15 — Modern transmission, not a single lost golden age

- S: Julian Strube, [Modern Śākta Identities in a Global Context](https://ochs.org.uk/lecture/modern-sakta-identities-in-a-global-context-bengali-tantrikas-and-john-woodroffe/), OCHS, 2018. **Lecture abstract read, recording not reviewed.** Nineteenth–twentieth century Bengal/global exchange; Woodroffe/Avalon and Indian collaborators. Confidence moderate, bounded to abstract thesis.
- S: SOAS, [Haṭha Yoga Project](https://www.soas.ac.uk/research/hatha-yoga-project-ancient-practices-modern-wellbeing), project description read; research and manuscript work challenge simplified histories. Detailed critical editions not read. Relevance: record who translated, selected and reframed the material; history remains an active research branch rather than a marketing explanation of decline.
