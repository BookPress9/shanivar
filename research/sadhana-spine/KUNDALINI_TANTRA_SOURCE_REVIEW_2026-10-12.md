# Kundalini Tantra — bounded source review for the Living Spine

Date: 12 October 2026. Status: **PROPOSED research input**, not accepted doctrine, clinical evidence or practice-release authorization.

## Source accessed

User-provided PDF: *Kundalini Tantra*, attributed in the filename to Swami Satyananda Saraswati. The PDF metadata reports the title *Introduction to Kundalini and Tantra*, author `Prof. A.K. Panda`, creation date 11 November 2007, and 284 PDF pages. This metadata conflict means the exact edition and scan provenance require bibliographic confirmation before formal citation. Full text was locally accessible. Relevant diagrams and surrounding pages were visually inspected; relevant passages were extracted across PDF pages 14–25, 45, 57–59, 73–89, 92, 101, 113, 117 and 129–130.

## What the book’s system says

- PDF pages 17–20 show a spinal chakra map and an ida/pingala/sushumna diagram. The presentation uses six principal chakras from mooladhara through ajna, plus bindu and sahasrara above them. It is not simply seven equal spinal checkpoints.
- PDF page 18 explicitly describes nadis as channels for consciousness rather than nerves. The same section assigns chakra correspondences to nerve plexuses, glands and organs.
- PDF page 20 places sushumna in the spinal canal and ida/pingala around the spinal cord, crossing at chakra points. It also asserts nostril/hemisphere relationships and new brain activation.
- PDF pages 20–21 use generator, cable and electrical-current analogies for upward movement through sushumna and claim kundalini is electrophysiological and measurable.
- PDF pages 24–25 acknowledge differing schools: kundalini as pranic flow without an anatomical counterpart versus nerve messages ascending through autonomic/spinal pathways. The text then speculates that cerebrospinal-fluid movement changes consciousness.
- PDF page 75 presents three granthis—brahma, vishnu and rudra—as symbolic obstacles or thresholds associated with regions around mooladhara, anahata and ajna.
- PDF pages 76–79 describe evolution through chakra stages, while also saying movement can pause, reverse, jump to sahasrara or occur in an individual chakra. This is not a clean linear level system.
- PDF pages 87–117 provide detailed chakra symbolism and physiological correspondences. Some passages distinguish a subtle centre from the biological organ; others make broad control or causal claims.
- PDF pages 129 onward contain practice instructions, including breath manipulation, counting, mudras, locks and concentration procedures. Their presence in the book does not make them appropriate for unattended delivery.

## Product translation

The Living Spine may use this source for a labelled **Satyananda/Bihar School teaching map** and for the concepts of a central axis, crossing channels, chakra regions and three granthi thresholds. It must not call that map anatomy or neuroscience.

The 33 vertebral progression is a Shanivar interaction model based on ordinary human vertebral segmentation (7 cervical, 12 thoracic, 5 lumbar, 5 fused sacral, typically 4 coccygeal), not a claim made by this PDF and not 33 spiritual ranks. Each completed reviewed practice can illuminate one segment as a factual continuity marker. Independent practice can do the same only when explicitly self-reported. Missed days remove nothing. Early ending is retained in history but does not complete a segment.

Chakra thresholds and granthi gates reveal contextual knowledge, source passages, adaptations and questions. They never unlock forceful breathwork, retention, mudras, bandhas, fasting, sexuality-related practice, altered-state induction or kundalini activation. Source study remains available directly.

The visual “energy” is named **practice current** in system logic. User-facing context can explain that traditional sources describe upward energy; the animation shows continued practice rather than detecting that event. The vagal/firefly layer responds only to an explicit self-report and remains labelled an illustration.
