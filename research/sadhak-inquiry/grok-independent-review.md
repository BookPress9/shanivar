## Claim-by-claim

### Color preference and association

**Supported, narrowly.** Stephen E. Palmer and Karen B. Schloss, *An ecological valence theory of human color preference*, PNAS 107(19), 8877–8882 (2010). Ledger SI-10: abstract and article discussion of the theory were reviewed on 2 October 2026; this pass did not reopen the paper. Recorded finding: associations with objects help explain preferences **in that study**. That is not evidence of a meditation effect, a chakra state, or a result for any Shanivar exercise.

**Shanivar synthesis, not a finding.** `research/sadhak-inquiry/README.md` proposes that a color can be an object of attention and a prompt for personal association, and offers a four-step everyday observation (ordinary light, usual breathing, eyes may stay open, “nothing” is a complete answer, stop if uneasy, no breath manipulation). Class: proposed product/research exercise. It is not experiential evidence that anyone did it or benefited. Not found: a usability study, a neutral-object comparison, or expert review. The README itself says those remain future and separate.

### High-ventilation breathwork

Founder item 3, preserved in `founder-research.txt`, summarizes a SciTechDaily article. It says high-ventilation breathwork evokes oceanic boundlessness and bliss **without adverse reactions**, and is a safe, accessible tool for reducing fear and negative emotion in mental health care. The ledger says that news article was not the audit source.

**Supported only inside SI-03’s recorded limits.** *Neurobiological substrates of altered states of consciousness induced by high ventilation breathwork accompanied by music*, PLOS ONE, 2025. Prior pass: methods, results/discussion, and limitations reviewed. README restatement, which this pass read in full:

- Experienced, screened practitioners; music; this setting.
- Altered-state intensity associated with autonomic and cerebral-perfusion changes.
- Authors report no adverse events in these sessions.
- Discomfort and panic-like symptoms in the scanner are also noted.
- Limited generalizability.
- Therapeutic mechanisms were not directly assessed with relevant clinical measures.
- No causal claim about sustained wellbeing follows.
- Not a beginner induction protocol.

**Contradicted / overstated:** “no adverse reactions,” “safe and accessible for everyone,” “lasting bliss,” “beginner Reel of fast breathing,” and any merger of these perfusion findings with Daoist qi, kundalini, or tummo. LM-002 and the calibration example “The paper reports no adverse events, so make a fast-breathing Reel for everyone” already reject that jump. This audit agrees with that correction and does not add a new reading of the paper.
